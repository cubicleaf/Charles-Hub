An Israeli human rights group that monitors violations against Palestinians says settler violence in the occupied West Bank is expanding beyond its traditional rural strongholds into larger, more populated Palestinian cities and towns. In a report released Friday titled "The Next Frontier," Yesh Din found that the establishment of new settlements and outposts throughout the West Bank has emboldened extremist settlers, who are now encroaching on communities that had previously been far removed from the front lines of settler violence.

The report ties the geographic spread directly to government policy, stating that "the uptick in violence corresponds with the Israeli government's approval of new settlements and the expansion of outposts that it doesn't authorize but often leaves in place." Yesh Din's authors described a consistent pattern: "As outposts advance deeper into Palestinian territory, violence, harassment and terror follow."

The findings land amid a broader West Bank crackdown that Prime Minister Netanyahu ordered in late July following an attack near Havat Gilad and a fatal confrontation in the village of Tal, and come the same day Israeli settlers and soldiers killed two Palestinians in separate incidents in Sa'ir and Jenin. Roughly 3 million Palestinians currently live under Israeli military rule in the West Bank, alongside about 560,000 Israeli settlers — a population imbalance the report suggests is now driving settler expansion, and associated violence, into territory it had not previously reached.
