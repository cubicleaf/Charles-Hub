The office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has publicly rejected both the entry of an international stabilization force into Gaza and any reconstruction of the Strip until Hamas is fully disarmed, pushing back directly on reports that political leadership had cleared the way for international peacekeepers. "Contrary to media reports, the political echelon has not approved the entry of the international force to the Gaza Strip," the statement said, adding, "Israel has made it clear that there will not be any reconstruction in the Strip before Hamas is fully disarmed."

The denial came after military officers from Burundi and Uganda toured Gaza sites with the Israel Defense Forces earlier in the week, which some reports characterized as the initial formation of an international stabilization force (ISF) under the Board of Peace framework brokered by President Trump. Trump had announced this month that "the Board of Peace reached a HISTORIC agreement for the COMPLETE DISARMAMENT of Hamas and all other armed groups in Gaza," describing it on Truth Social as "a critical step towards Gaza finally being governed by a new Palestinian government that will work closely with the Board of Peace to help the Palestinian people." Hamas has pressed Washington to compel Israeli compliance with the broader deal, which envisions a phased Israeli withdrawal alongside Hamas disarmament.

Netanyahu has separately and explicitly rejected the disarmament proposal presented to Israel's cabinet. "Israel does not accept the 15-point document," he told cabinet members earlier this month, saying the military "will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas is disarmed … it means heavy weaponry, lighter weaponry, all weaponry." He went on to draw a distinction between the disarmament Israel is willing to accept and what has so far been offered: "we are talking about genuine disarmament, not fictitious disarmament. [The US] have ideas, some of which are acceptable to us and some of which are unacceptable to us."

The rejection underscores a widening gap between the Board of Peace's stated progress on the disarmament roadmap and Israel's own conditions for withdrawal and reconstruction, leaving the fate of Gaza's postwar governance and rebuilding effort unresolved even as Trump continues to publicly describe the framework as a breakthrough.
