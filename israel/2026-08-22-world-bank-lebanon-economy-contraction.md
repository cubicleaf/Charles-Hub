Lebanon's economy is projected to contract by 6.4 percent in 2026 as the ongoing conflict with Israel reverses what had been the country's strongest post-crisis recovery, the World Bank said Friday. The finding, published in the bank's Summer 2026 Lebanon Economic Monitor titled "A Conflict-Torn Economy," follows a year in which Lebanon's real GDP had grown 4.2 percent in 2025 — its highest growth rate since the 2019 financial collapse.

"The rebound was sharply interrupted by the March 2026 escalation in conflict, which further damaged housing and infrastructure, displaced communities, disrupted supply chains and weighed heavily on tourism and domestic demand," the report said, referring to the intensified Israeli military campaign that began that month. "Real GDP is projected to contract by 6.4 percent in 2026, reflecting the collapse in tourism, weaker consumption, disrupted supply chains, heightened insecurity, and prolonged displacement," it added.

Consumer prices are also under renewed pressure, with inflation expected to accelerate to 17.5 percent, which the World Bank attributed to supply disruptions, elevated shipping costs and volatile fuel prices. Dahlia Khalifa, the World Bank's division director for the Middle East, said at the report's launch that "advancing reforms, particularly on banking sector restructuring and fiscal management, will be critical to restoring confidence, protecting stability, and mobilising the financing needed for reconstruction and recovery."

The economic toll adds a quantified dimension to Israel's ongoing military campaign in southern Lebanon, which has continued in violation of the ceasefire announced in mid-2026 and has already produced mounting civilian casualties and repeated confrontations with UNIFIL peacekeepers. Lebanon's economy has been in crisis since a 2019 banking collapse, and the International Monetary Fund said it plans to resume technical meetings in Beirut next month to evaluate further structural policy measures toward a formal bailout program.
