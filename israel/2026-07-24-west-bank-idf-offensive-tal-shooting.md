Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz ordered a "wide-scale military operation" across Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank on 24 July 2026, following an attack near the settlement of Havat Gilad that killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three others, according to Al Jazeera. The same day, a separate deadly confrontation took place in the Palestinian village of Tal, southwest of Nablus. Footage of that incident shows armed settlers confronting a crowd of Palestinians; in one clip, a Palestinian man is seen seizing a rifle from a settler and fatally shooting him in what Al Jazeera described as an apparent act of self-defense. Israeli soldiers then shot and killed that Palestinian man, and three other Palestinians also died in the confrontation. Israelis are barred from entering Tal, which is under Palestinian Authority control, and both sides dispute what happened there.

By Saturday morning, Israeli forces had arrested nearly 50 Palestinians in Tal — where Friday's shooting took place — and raided roughly 70 homes there, in what the military described as an "extensive" operation. Separately, several arrests were made in the city of Jenin and in towns and villages across that governorate, where Israeli forces raided multiple homes and deployed large numbers of troops. In the Nablus governorate, Israeli settlers set fire to Palestinian homes and other property in several communities, according to sources and witnesses cited by Al Jazeera.

The Palestinian Health Ministry says at least 87 Palestinians have been killed by Israelis across the West Bank since the start of 2026, 21 of them by settlers. Critics say settler violence has been emboldened both by the absence of international pressure to end the Gaza war and by the rhetoric of far-right Israeli ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich.

UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese condemned the violence, describing it on social media as "pogroms against defenseless civilians all over the West Bank, while Gaza is under fire." She also said: "Arms embargo now — stop trade now."

Human Rights Watch also urged the Israeli government to intervene, warning that "years of lawlessness and impunity for attacks have turned the West Bank into a powder keg waiting to explode, and decisive action is urgently needed."
