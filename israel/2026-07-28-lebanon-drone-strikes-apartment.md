Israeli drones struck an apartment complex in the southern Lebanese city of Nabatieh al-Fawqa on 28 July 2026, causing substantial damage and setting the building on fire, Antiwar.com reported; the number of casualties had not been confirmed. Bombing and demolition operations continued elsewhere across the south the same day, with explosions reported in Bint Jbeil and Majdal Zoun and a large blast from Israeli forces in Qusayr. The strikes came despite recent Israel-Lebanon agreements presented publicly as peace deals — arrangements that, as Antiwar.com noted, "didn't actual require Israel to stop attacking nor withdraw from Lebanon."

UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk had warned a day earlier that the Israeli demolitions and population displacements could amount to war crimes. Separately, France's representative to the UN Security Council issued a statement calling on Israel to immediately withdraw from Lebanon, while reaffirming support for UNIFIL peacekeepers after the recent death of one of their members.

The death toll from the ongoing Israeli campaign has eased somewhat in recent days even as strikes and demolitions continue, and large numbers of Lebanese remain displaced. A new round of Israel-Lebanon talks is scheduled to begin early next week, though it follows repeated Israeli statements ruling out withdrawal — making it, in Antiwar.com's assessment, unlikely to change conditions for those still living under the campaign.
