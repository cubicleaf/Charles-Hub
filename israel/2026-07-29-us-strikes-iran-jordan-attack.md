US Central Command announced on the night of 29 July 2026 that it had begun launching new airstrikes against Iran, hours after President Donald Trump threatened retaliation for an Iranian missile attack on a US base in Jordan, according to Antiwar.com. CENTCOM described the strikes as a "powerful response" to the Jordan attack, though the full extent of the operation remained unclear. Trump vowed earlier in the day that the US would be "hitting them hard" and that Iran was "going to get a beating," using profanity-laced language in comments to a Fox News reporter. He called the Jordan strike a "surprise attack" that gave US troops only "minutes" to intercept incoming missiles.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said resistance would continue as long as "threats against the Islamic Republic of Iran" and "illegal and mischievous actions" by American forces persisted. The new strikes followed several days of lull in direct US-Iran combat, though the US naval blockade of Iranian ports never lifted; Iran had warned that the continued blockade amounted to an "expansion of war in the region." During the earlier phase of fighting this month, Iranian strikes on US bases in Jordan killed at least three American troops and wounded more than 100, according to Antiwar.com's reporting.

The escalation came after Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu met Trump at the White House to discuss the Iran war, a meeting Netanyahu afterward called "one of the best conversations I've had with our friend, US President Trump," describing "full cooperation" and a "joint goal that Iran won't have nuclear weapons." The renewed strikes reopen active combat after a lull, with Iran having already warned that the ongoing US naval blockade of its ports amounted to an "expansion of war in the region."
