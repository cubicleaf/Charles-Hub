Khalil al-Hayya, elected as Hamas's new political bureau chief days earlier, delivered his first televised address as leader on 22 July 2026, broadcast on the group's Al-Aqsa TV channel. Speaking at a lectern flanked by the flags of Palestine and Hamas, he pledged to rebuild Gaza, free political prisoners held in Israel, and work toward uniting Gaza, the West Bank, and Jerusalem, while saying Hamas would resist attempts to force Palestinians out of Gaza. Al Jazeera reported that he also thanked the countries trying to end what the outlet described as Israel's "genocidal war."

Al-Hayya was chosen by Hamas's General Shura Council on 20 July, defeating former political chief Khaled Meshaal in a run-off vote. He succeeds Yahya Sinwar, who was killed by the Israeli army in Gaza in 2024; Hamas had been governed by a five-man collective leadership, of which al-Hayya was a member, in the interim.

In the speech, al-Hayya restated that Hamas's primary goal is a Palestinian state encompassing Gaza and the Israeli-occupied West Bank, with its capital in East Jerusalem — a position that has been the group's official objective since a revised policy document published in 2017.

Al-Hayya called on international mediators and the wider international community to press Israel to follow the terms of the US-backed ceasefire agreed in October 2025, which Al Jazeera reported Israel has largely ignored. He said: "We appreciate the role of the mediators in Egypt and Qatar and Turkiye who are exerting every effort to stop the cascade of bloodletting in the Gaza Strip." Al Jazeera noted that while al-Hayya thanked the countries that have sought to mediate and end the war, he did not mention the United States.

The address served as both a leadership introduction and a restatement of Hamas's existing demands on statehood, prisoners, and reconstruction, alongside a call for the international community to press Israel over the ceasefire terms Al Jazeera reported it has largely ignored.
