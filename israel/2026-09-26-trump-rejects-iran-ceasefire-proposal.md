United States President Donald Trump has reportedly ruled out Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week, according to unnamed US officials cited by the Wall Street Journal. The rejection came hours after Iran's Foreign Minister, Abbas Araghchi, said the proposal called for the US to take actions it had committed to under the failed June 17 Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding on an accelerated timetable: releasing $12bn in frozen Iranian assets, lifting Iranian oil sanctions, and ending the US naval blockade on Iranian ports, while halting all hostilities, including in Lebanon. Araghchi said that once US conditions were met — a process he estimated would take four or five days — the strait, through which one-fifth of global oil and gas shipments pass in peacetime, could reopen and negotiations toward a "final deal" would begin on day seven. "The choice now rests with the United States," Araghchi said. "Iran does not accept coercion, threats or intimidation."

The US government has not publicly responded to the plan, but the Journal reported that Trump had quickly dismissed it, citing officials who said he was skeptical Iran would fulfill US demands — chiefly that Iran never acquire a nuclear weapon and that free passage through Hormuz be restored. Late Friday, Trump posted a map on Truth Social labeling the waterway "Trump Strait," reiterating an earlier claim that the US has "total control" over it, which Iran has strongly denied. Despite the impasse, indirect diplomacy continued at the UN General Assembly in New York, where Qatar has been relaying messages between the two sides; US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff held mediated talks with Iranian counterparts earlier in the week that Trump described as "very good."

According to the officials cited by the Journal, Trump told aides he expects to launch a new bombing campaign against Iran after the November US midterm elections, a comment that follows his warning from the UN General Assembly podium that he was weighing whether to "annihilate" Iran and "drive them into hell" absent a deal. Analysts said the midterms are likely to shape his calculus: Georgetown University's Paul Musgrave said Trump "doesn't want to end the war on unfavourable terms," and that a poor Republican showing could push him toward "unilateral actions, including perhaps a resumption of warfare," while the Atlantic Council's Thomas Warrick said there remains "an enormous amount of scepticism in Washington that the two countries are on the same page."
