Hamas announced on 31 July 2026 that it had agreed to a phased roadmap for disarming in Gaza, a deal intended to move the war toward a full end after nearly three years, Al Jazeera reported. The agreement, confirmed Thursday and Friday by the United States and mediators from Egypt, Qatar and Turkiye, requires Hamas to make provisions to disarm and surrender its weapons in stages while Israel withdraws its forces — the long-delayed next phase of the ceasefire struck in October 2025, which reduced but did not end the fighting. President Donald Trump, who unveiled his 20-point Gaza peace plan last year, hailed the agreement as "a historic agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas and all other armed groups in Gaza," calling it a "monumental step toward lasting peace and security." Writing separately on Truth Social, he framed it as "a critical step towards Gaza finally being governed by a new Palestinian government that will work closely with the Board of Peace," and said Israel is "very happy" with the agreement.

The Israeli government had issued no official response by Friday evening, but far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir rejected the draft outright, calling it "unacceptable to Israel." "The assassinations in Gaza must continue, the encouragement of immigration must happen, Israel must win," he said. Gadi Eisenkot, the retired IDF chief of staff now positioning himself as a leading contender to replace Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, warned that "the State of Israel must not accept a reality in which Hamas survives, rearms and waits for the opportunity to carry out the next massacre."

Hamas conditioned its own compliance on a genuine Israeli withdrawal, saying ending the killing in Gaza is "the fundamental prerequisite" before a timeline for implementation can be established. Negotiator Ghazi Hamad told Al Jazeera the deal was a "comprehensive package" rather than a straightforward weapons handover, encompassing an end to Israel's offensive, a full troop withdrawal, deployment of the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza and an international stabilization force, and reconstruction. Hamas said it "made concessions for the sake of our Palestinian people." Palestinian Islamic Jihad, a party to the original ceasefire, disputed that a deal had been finalized, saying it had "reservations about it in its currently circulated form."

Nickolay Mladenov, director-general of the Trump-backed Board of Peace who helped architect the phased approach, acknowledged the talks had nearly collapsed repeatedly, saying "there were several points when I did not believe we would make it," and stressed that implementation and verification "must be real and effective." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas offered cautious welcome, writing that "a lot needs to fall in place for this to work."
