The UK's Charity Commission has reminded a British charity that funds the Board of Deputies of British Jews of the risks that arise when a charity "is, or is perceived to be, closely associated with a company," following a protest against a London real estate event that promoted properties in illegal Israeli settlements, Middle East Eye reported.

The regulatory guidance to trustees of the Board of Deputies Charitable Foundation came in response to a complaint from the International Centre of Justice for Palestinians (ICJP). The ICJP said the Board had claimed a protest demonstration outside Edgware United Synagogue — which hosted the Great Israeli Real Estate Event — was organized on "bogus grounds," relying on assurances from organizers that settlement properties would not be marketed. The Board characterized the protest as an attempt to "harass and intimidate members of the Jewish community," even though the event's organizers later apologized for including properties in the occupied West Bank and East Jerusalem in their promotional brochure.

The Commission confirmed it had issued advice and guidance to the charity's trustees under section 15(2) of the Charities Act 2011, reminding them of the risks that arise when a charity's public statements are closely tied to a company's conduct. It said it would retain the information the ICJP provided and could factor it into future regulatory engagement with the charity.

ICJP legal researcher Shaima Dallali said "Palestinians are being driven from their land while that same land is marketed to buyers abroad, including in the UK," adding that the properties promoted at the event were "part of the economic infrastructure that sustains Israel's unlawful occupation and settlement enterprise."

The advice follows an earlier Charity Commission statement on 13 August criticizing trustees of Edgware United Synagogue itself for hosting the event despite what the Commission described as their own misgivings and an organization-wide decision not to permit it.
