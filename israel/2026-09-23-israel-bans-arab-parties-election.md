Israel's Central Elections Committee voted on 23 September 2026 to bar the country's two main Arab-majority political lists from the 27 October parliamentary election, a decision that could leave Palestinian citizens of Israel — about one-fifth of the population — without their principal representatives in the Knesset. The committee, chaired by a Supreme Court justice but voted on by sitting Knesset parties, disqualified the Joint List, an alliance of Hadash, Ta'al and Balad, by a vote of 18-5, and Ra'am (the United Arab List), also holding five seats and led by Mansour Abbas, by the same margin. It separately voted 18-5 to bar Ofer Cassif, a Jewish Hadash lawmaker, and 31-4 to bar Balad chairman Sami Abu Shehadeh.

The petitions, brought by Likud and Itamar Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit, accused the parties and candidates of denying Israel's existence as a Jewish and democratic state, backing armed struggle, or inciting racism. The case against Abu Shehadeh centered in part on an October 8, 2023 article he wrote on the Arab 48 website describing Hamas's attack the previous day as "an important historic event militarily, politically and strategically" — wording Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said could amount to support for armed struggle. Abu Shehadeh has said he opposes armed struggle and supports a "political and civil struggle for peace, justice, and equality." Baharav-Miara opposed disqualifying the two lists and Cassif, saying the evidence against them did not meet the legal threshold.

Petitions to disqualify the far-right parties of National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich — both of whom face international sanctions over their rhetoric and policies toward Palestinians — were rejected by the same committee. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who faces an International Criminal Court arrest warrant and an ongoing domestic corruption trial, also remains eligible to run.

Cassif called the ruling "another nail in the coffin of the remnants of democracy" in Israel and said he and the Joint List would be in the next Knesset regardless. Adalah, the Palestinian legal rights group representing the disqualified lists, plans to challenge the rulings before the Supreme Court, which has overturned previous attempts to exclude Arab parties and has final say before the election. Human rights advocate Ubai Al-Aboudi told Al Jazeera the vote went further than past exclusion attempts: "It is the first time that all representation has been barred," he said, calling it a sign of "the mentality and extremism of the current Israeli political structure."
