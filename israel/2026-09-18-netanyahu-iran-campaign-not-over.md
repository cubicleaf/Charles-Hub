Israel signaled on 18 September 2026 that it was not ready to end its military campaign against Iran, even as China pressed both sides for restraint. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel still had work to complete against Iran and its regional allies and that it intended to bring down Iran's regime, telling his official account: "We have done most of the work, but there is still work to complete, and we will complete it." Defense Minister Israel Katz said separately that Israel still had "important goals" to achieve against Iran and in other arenas, and that the broader campaign was not over, adding: "We will act with military force and political wisdom to achieve them."

President Donald Trump said he was weighing a return to large-scale strikes on Iran, telling an interviewer published the same day: "I have a big decision coming up. Do I want to go in and annihilate them [the Iranian regime] or do I not? It's a big decision. Anything could happen with me." He is expected to meet leaders of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman at the United Nations General Assembly the following week before deciding on the war's next phase.

China intensified its own diplomacy, with Foreign Minister Wang Yi meeting Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Beijing to urge restraint, a return to substantive talks, and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, before separately speaking with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Underscoring the international rift over the conflict, Russia and China vetoed a US-drafted UN Security Council resolution to extend the mandate of the panel monitoring sanctions on Iran. The toll on shipping was stark: only three commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, down from 12 the day before and well below the recent 10-day average of about 17, according to preliminary ship-tracking data.
