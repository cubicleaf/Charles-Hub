A trial of nine Palestine Action activists, known as the Instro 10, is due to begin on 5 October 2026 at London's Old Bailey and last eight weeks, according to an account published on 1 October by The Grayzone. The defendants are accused of criminal damage, burglary including trespass and criminal damage, and violent disorder over a 17 June 2024 break-in at the Instro Precision factory, a subsidiary of Israeli arms maker Elbit Systems. The report says the intrusion caused damage reportedly in excess of £1 million and that an Instro export licence to Israel was cancelled two days later.

The Grayzone's central claim is about how the case will be run. It says the jury and public are not permitted to know, and the British media is prohibited from reporting, that a conviction could be sentenced as a terror offence, because of a "terrorist connection" label. The outlet notes that in June four activists in a similar case received prison terms ranging from nearly five to over eight years, and that the judge there prohibited the defendants from discussing their motives. The Grayzone presents this as stacking the deck against defendants; these characterisations are the outlet's, and the article carries no response from prosecutors or the court.

The article also says a British court dropped charges of aggravated burglary against the group, which it reads as showing prosecutors lacked evidence of intent to harm anyone. It further reports that The Grayzone reviewed heavily redacted records of a meeting between prosecutors and counter-terror police, held within 10 days of the Instro action, that discussed the resource implications of proscribing the group.

The UK proscribed Palestine Action as a terrorist entity in July 2025.
