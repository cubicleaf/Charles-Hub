A Fort Lauderdale, Florida, venue cancelled a scheduled rally featuring Democratic US Representative Rashida Tlaib after a state lawmaker who chairs the Florida Legislative Jewish Caucus publicly branded the event an "anti-Jew" gathering, Reuters reported via Middle East Eye — the latest flashpoint in the Democratic Party's internal divisions over Israel.

Tlaib, the first Palestinian American woman elected to Congress, had been scheduled to appear Friday at a rally in support of three candidates running in Florida's upcoming Democratic primary. The Venue Fort Lauderdale, which had booked the event, abruptly cancelled it after State Representative Michael Gottlieb posted a statement Thursday describing the planned rally in those terms.

The Venue said in its own statement that it was cancelling over "increased security concerns," adding that it is Jewish-owned and that "our values and commitment to the Jewish community are deeply important to us."

Tlaib has repeatedly condemned antisemitism while maintaining that criticism of Israel's occupation of Palestinian territories and its military campaigns in Gaza and Lebanon does not amount to antisemitism — a distinction that has become one of the most contested fault lines in American political debate over the war, increasingly playing out not just in Congress but at the level of individual venues deciding whether pro-Palestinian events can proceed at all.
