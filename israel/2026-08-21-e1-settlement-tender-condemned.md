Israel has opened government tenders for 1,234 new homes in the E1 settlement area of the occupied West Bank, advancing a long-contested project that rights groups and Western governments say would sever the northern and southern West Bank from each other and foreclose the possibility of a contiguous Palestinian state. The tender, opened by Israel's Ministry of Construction and Housing, covers part of a wider plan for 3,400 homes on about 12 square kilometres of land east of Jerusalem, linking the settlement of Ma'ale Adumim to the city and creating what analysts describe as a continuous Israeli settlement corridor cutting the West Bank in two. Bids close on 19 October 2026, eight days before Israeli elections scheduled for 27 October.

Plans for E1 date to 1991 under Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir and were repeatedly frozen amid US and European pressure, most recently in 2022. Analysts say President Trump's second term has given Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's government, which depends on far-right coalition partners, a freer hand to revive the project. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, who oversees the West Bank Settlement Administration, has said the project's approval "buries the idea of a Palestinian state."

The move prompted a joint statement from the leaders of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Canada and Norway calling the tender decision "unacceptable" and warning it would "undermine the prospect of the two-state solution by driving a wedge through the West Bank and harming the territorial contiguity of the Palestinian Territories." The governments directly warned companies against bidding, citing risk of "legal and reputational consequences." UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said Britain had summoned Israel's chargé d'affaires and demanded the tenders be reversed. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir dismissed the objection on social media, telling Miliband that "the British Mandate for the Land of Israel ended in 1948."

About 3,700 Palestinians, predominantly Bedouin, live across 18 communities in E1's path, including Khan al-Ahmar, which has faced a longstanding threat of demolition. The tenders were published while a legal challenge from Ir Amim, Peace Now and Bimkom remains pending before the Jerusalem District Court. Palestinian advocate Ubai al-Aboudi said the project would deliver a "final nail in the coffin" of the two-state solution, adding that "Israel can live with all the verbal condemnation in the world as long as it's advancing realities on the ground." Ines Abdel Razek of the Palestine Institute for Public Diplomacy said Western statements about a two-state solution amounted to "diplomatic rhetoric used to cover deliberate inaction."
