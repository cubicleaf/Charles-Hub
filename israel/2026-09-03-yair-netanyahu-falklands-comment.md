Comments by Yair Netanyahu, the son of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, backing Argentina's claim to the British-controlled Falkland Islands have caused an unlikely row with Britain's right wing — one of the UK's last reliably pro-Israel political constituencies, Al Jazeera reported on 3 September 2026. The younger Netanyahu waded into the longrunning territorial dispute over the South Atlantic archipelago last week, writing on X in Spanish: "The Falkland Islands belong to Argentina." In follow-up posts he said Israel should back Argentina's claim because Argentina's far-right government under President Javier Milei is more friendly to Israel, and he accused the UK of "hypocrisy and hostility towards Israel" over its recognition of Palestinian statehood, as well as of perpetuating "antisemitism" and "blood libels."

The remarks, apparently intended as a jab at the UK's Labour government, instead angered figures on the British right who are typically pro-Israel. Rupert Lowe, head of the far-right Restore Britain party, responded directly on X that the Falklands would "remain" British and that a UK government led by his party would "go to war if necessary to ensure it," adding, "You have enough problems to be focusing on in Israel." Restore currently polls at just 3 percent, according to YouGov. Former Conservative MP Sir Conor Burns called Yair Netanyahu's comments "stupid" and warned they would be "damaging to Israel's cause on the [British] right." No Israeli government official has publicly endorsed the younger Netanyahu's position.

Analysts quoted by Al Jazeera framed the episode as symptomatic of deeper strain in UK-Israel relations, rooted in disagreements over Gaza, the West Bank, and the wider regional conflict, and of the outsized informal influence Yair Netanyahu is seen to wield given his closeness to his father. Israeli officials have previously invoked the Falklands and other Western territorial disputes, including Spain's African enclave of Ceuta, when responding to criticism of Israel's own settlement policy in the occupied West Bank.
