President Donald Trump said on 23 July 2026 that he is seriously considering a major new escalation against Iran, telling Axios he was weighing combat operations that could include strikes larger than those carried out during "Operation Epic Fury." "I am considering a massive attack. Bigger than ever before. I am close to making a decision. We are all set for it," Trump said, adding that he had not yet made a final decision.

Trump specifically addressed Israel's potential role in any expanded operation. He said Israel "would join in two minutes if I ask them to," but insisted "we don't need anybody" to launch a new strike against Iran. He also said there would be "consequences" for Israel if it joined US strikes — a remark reported by the Irish Times as hinting at the risk of Iranian retaliation against Israel.

The comments came nearly five months after the United States and Israel attacked Iran with the stated goal of thwarting its nuclear ambitions, with no sign yet of a diplomatic off-ramp. The US military completed its 12th consecutive night of strikes on Iran that Wednesday. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it had struck US military targets in Jordan and Kuwait, including a communications tower, saying the attacks were retaliation for US strikes on equivalent Iranian infrastructure. Iranian-aligned Houthi forces in Yemen also claimed strikes on two Saudi oil tankers, in a blockade that threatens a second chokepoint for global shipping alongside the Strait of Hormuz.

Separately, the Republican-led House of Representatives passed legislation directing Trump to halt military action against Iran, in what the Irish Times described as the latest symbolic rebuke of the president from Congress. The vote passed 214-208, with four Republicans joining Democrats in favor. Such resolutions have passed repeatedly during the conflict without leading to a halt in operations.

A ceasefire deal reached between the US and Iran last month has unravelled in recent weeks, with the Strait of Hormuz as the focal point of the dispute. Trump's willingness to openly discuss both a bigger offensive and Israel's conditional role in it underscores how central Israel remains to the conflict, even as the president frames the current campaign as one the US can escalate without direct Israeli participation.
