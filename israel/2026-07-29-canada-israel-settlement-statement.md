Israel's Foreign Ministry accused Canada of moving to "embolden terror" on 29 July 2026, after Global Affairs Canada issued a statement reaffirming Ottawa's opposition to Israeli settlement expansion in the occupied West Bank, Al Jazeera reported. Canada's statement said "the expansion of Israeli settlements in the West Bank must stop," calling the settlements illegal under international law and a threat to "peace, security and stability," and called for de-escalation and protection of civilians under international humanitarian law. The exchange played out largely on social media, with Israel's Foreign Ministry responding that "a wave of Palestinian terrorism is sweeping Judea and Samaria" — using the biblical term for the West Bank favored by Israeli settlers — and accusing Canada of condemning Israel rather than "the terrorists."

The statement came the same day the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) said it was "alarmed" by Israel's announced plans to expand settlements and outposts, as well as by calls from Israeli leaders for "revenge and collective punishment" against Palestinian communities. The UN reports that 76 Palestinians have died in settler attacks so far this year, and that it recorded roughly 190 such attacks in each of the first four months of 2026 — a pace that, if it continues, would put the year's total near 2,000, surpassing 2025's total of 1,835.

The diplomatic clash follows a surge in West Bank violence after a Palestinian man in the village of Tal grabbed a settler's gun and shot him dead during a settler incursion into the village last week, an incident Israeli authorities have used as a pretext for a sweeping crackdown on Palestinian communities. Canada and Israel have feuded before over the West Bank: in December, Israel denied entry to a group of Canadian members of parliament who had planned to meet Palestinian officials and human rights advocates there. Critics note that Ottawa has rarely followed its statements with concrete policy steps against Israel.
