The World Health Organization said on Friday 2 October 2026 that repeated delays in bringing medicines, prosthetics and other supplies from Israel into Gaza risk "devastating consequences" for Palestinians who urgently need them, according to Al Jazeera's report.

Reinhilde Van De Weerdt, the WHO's representative in Palestine, called on Israeli authorities to "release the pressure on medical supplies" and said essential medicines should not be treated as dual-use items. She said one shipment of prosthetics and assistive devices had been rejected four times over the past six months, adding that every rejection means another delay for someone who has lost a limb. Medicines, by contrast, move relatively well, she said, with almost 87 percent approved and just over 1 percent rejected.

The WHO said approved aid can enter Gaza only through Kerem Shalom at the southern tip of the territory, while Rafah is open only for limited movement of people, including patients needing treatment abroad. Four other main entry points, Kissufim, Gate 96, Erez and Erez West, remain closed and under total Israeli control. Van De Weerdt asked for unimpeded and predictable entry of humanitarian and health supplies at scale, and for a multi-crossing model.

She warned that as winter begins almost two million people are in need of shelter against the cold and the risk of flooding, and pointed to a heightened risk of preventable disease outbreaks tied to unsafe shelter and water, and to rats whose bites transmit infection, a threat harder to track after the destruction of public health laboratories. The UN humanitarian office OCHA warned a day earlier of public health risks from accumulating solid waste in Gaza City.

Al Jazeera describes the situation as almost 12 months into a fragile ceasefire between Hamas and Israel marked by continuing Israeli air attacks, with OCHA calling the humanitarian situation overwhelming. The report carries no Israeli government response.
