Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has called for an end to the months-long war with the United States, arguing that Tehran now holds a position of strength even as diplomatic talks remain stalled. "It is better that we bring the war to an end now as we are in a position of power and dignity," Pezeshkian said in a meeting with doctors on 21 August 2026. "The whole world acknowledges our victory and emphasises that America has attacked our schools, hospitals and infrastructure in violation of all regulations and is hated around the world."

Pezeshkian, whose authority is ultimately subordinate to Iran's Supreme Leader, also defended the June memorandum of understanding reached with Washington against hardline domestic critics in parliament who have accused his administration of making concessions. "They cannot find even a single clause in this agreement that indicates capitulation. All the commitments concern the other side," he said. Even so, Iran's military leadership signaled continued readiness for confrontation: armed forces chief of staff Major-General Ali Abdollahi said Iran would respond to any new threats "with crushing, punishing and devastating responses" across land, sea, air, air defense and cyberspace.

The competing messages came as Oman's and Iran's foreign ministers held a call to discuss conditions for resuming negotiations, while Tehran continues to keep the Strait of Hormuz partially shut and Washington persists with its naval counter-blockade. President Trump, asked whether US military options with Iran were limited, said only "we're seeing what happens," adding: "We have total control of that entire region having to do with the Strait of Hormuz, and that means well into it, the land areas." He also warned of economic consequences for any country offering Iran a "lifeline."

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington would move to "collapse" the Iranian government through a new sanctions campaign, to be detailed at a Monday press conference, marking a shift toward economic rather than military pressure as the war approaches its six-month mark. Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned the plan as "economic terrorism," while China's Foreign Ministry said "sanctions and pressure will not help resolve the issue."
