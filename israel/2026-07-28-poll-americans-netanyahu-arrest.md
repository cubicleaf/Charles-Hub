Nearly half of Americans want Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu arrested by US authorities during his visit to Washington, according to an Economist/YouGov poll cited by the Israeli newspaper Haaretz and reported by Antiwar.com on 28 July 2026. The poll found 49% of Americans favored Netanyahu's arrest, including 74% of Kamala Harris voters and 23% of those who supported Donald Trump. It also found that 54% of Americans view Netanyahu unfavorably and 47% believe he is guilty of war crimes and has damaged US-Israel relations. A separate YouGov poll published five days earlier, on 23 July, found comparable partisan splits, with 68% of Democrats and 28% of Republicans favoring Netanyahu's arrest if he entered the US.

The polling followed comments from New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, who called Netanyahu a "war criminal" and urged federal authorities to arrest him to enforce the International Criminal Court's outstanding warrant for his role in war crimes in Gaza. President Trump rejected the demand outright, saying Netanyahu "will not be arrested, in any way, shape, or form, while in the United States of America."

The arrest question surfaced as Netanyahu was in Washington for a string of high-profile engagements: he met Trump and senior administration officials at the White House on Tuesday, where he was expected to push for the US to restart strikes on Iran and escalate the war, and was due to attend the funeral of Senator Lindsey Graham, a staunch supporter of the US-Israel relationship. The visit placed Netanyahu on US soil despite the outstanding ICC warrant against him, testing how the Trump administration would handle a sitting ally facing an international arrest order — and underscoring a widening gap between elite US-Israel diplomacy and American public opinion, where a plurality now says the Israeli prime minister should face arrest.
