A group of US Senate Democrats introduced legislation on 23 September 2026 seeking sanctions against facilitators of Israel's "E1" settlement project, which would link the Ma'ale Adumim settlement and other West Bank settlements to occupied East Jerusalem. The bill, co-sponsored by 19 Democrats and spearheaded by Senators Chris Coons of Delaware, Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, Ron Wyden of Oregon and Ruben Gallego of Arizona, would direct the Treasury Secretary to sanction "any foreign person" found responsible for or complicit in E1 construction, including anyone who submits a bid for E1 tenders or helps organize, finance or facilitate the transfer of Israeli civilians into the settlements. "As Palestinian civilians face a campaign of devastation and fear from violent extremist settlers, the United States must send a strong message that this behaviour is unacceptable," Coons said in a statement.

The E1 project, if completed, would sever East Jerusalem — long envisioned as the capital of a future Palestinian state — from the rest of the West Bank while cleaving Palestinian territory in two, an outcome supporters and critics alike have described as a probable death blow to Palestinian statehood. Criticism intensified last month after Israel invited bids for 1,200 new housing units in the area. British Foreign Minister Ed Miliband called the project an "unacceptable and destructive act," and the UN Human Rights Office described it as a "grave and unlawful step to consolidate annexation." The Trump administration has not directly addressed the E1 plan but has reiterated general opposition to Israeli annexation of the West Bank.

The bill is the latest sign of growing unease among congressional Democrats over Israeli policy, ahead of November's midterm elections in which the party is seeking to retake both chambers of Congress. More than 20 Senate Democrats have also recently introduced a separate resolution calling for an investigation into surging West Bank violence, in which at least nine US citizens have been killed. A growing number of Democrats have voted in recent months to block arms transfers to Israel, and a handful of Republicans have also begun to question the terms of unconditional US military aid.
