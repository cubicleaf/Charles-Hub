President Donald Trump said on 23 July 2026 that a newly announced civilian nuclear cooperation agreement between the United States and Saudi Arabia is "totally subject" to the kingdom normalizing relations with Israel through the Abraham Accords, throwing a deal unveiled only a day earlier into uncertainty.

In a post on his Truth Social account, Trump wrote that the agreement "will be approved, but is totally subject to Saudi Arabia joining the very respected and successful Abraham Accords." Al Jazeera noted that any Saudi acceptance of normalizing ties with Israel under the accords would represent a major pivot for Riyadh, which has long opposed such a prospect. Saudi Arabia did not immediately respond to Trump's statement. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters she did not believe Trump had spoken with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman since posting the condition, though he had raised the issue in previous conversations, and she maintained that if Riyadh does not join the Abraham Accords, "the deal is off."

The condition followed the US Department of Energy's announcement a day earlier that Washington and Riyadh had signed "a peaceful nuclear cooperation agreement for a decades-long, multi-billion-dollar partnership," along with a "bilateral safeguard agreement" tied to launching a Saudi civilian nuclear programme.

Trump also moved to tamp down reports that the deal would allow Saudi Arabia to enrich uranium domestically rather than import nuclear fuel. "There will be no enrichment of material!" he wrote, adding: "The United States is not opposed to Civil (Non-Enriched) Nuclear Facilities." Several lawmakers had warned the agreement could fuel a nuclear arms race in the Middle East, particularly if it allowed domestic enrichment, and some warned it could incentivize Iran to seek nuclear weapons of its own.

The enrichment dispute carries particular weight given the war the US and Israel have waged against Iran since 28 February, which Al Jazeera reported has been justified in significant part by Iran's own uranium enrichment program. The Abraham Accords were signed in 2020 by the UAE and Bahrain and later joined by Morocco. Both Trump and the administration of former President Joe Biden have for years pushed for Saudi Arabia to join the accords.
