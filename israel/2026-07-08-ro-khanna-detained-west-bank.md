US Representative Ro Khanna, a California Democrat, said he was detained for more than an hour by armed Israeli settlers and soldiers on 8 July 2026 while traveling to visit a Palestinian village in the occupied West Bank. Khanna said armed settlers stopped his van for about 20 minutes before Israeli soldiers arrived and continued blocking the road; the standoff was resolved only after he contacted the US embassy in Israel.

Khanna described the episode in blunt terms: "What happened was unprecedented. They had violent settlers detain American citizens, including an American government official. You had these settlers brandishing M4s [rifles], kicking the tyres of our van, laughing at us, mocking at us, videotaping us." The Israeli military disputed his account, saying it had merely "dispersed" civilians who were blocking the road, rather than detaining Khanna's delegation.

Israeli officials have declined to apologize or commit to holding the settlers accountable, and several went further, criticizing Khanna directly. Israel's ambassador to the US, Michael Leiter, suggested Khanna had brought the treatment on himself by failing to coordinate the trip with the Israeli government, telling CBS News that Khanna "decided to coordinate his trip not with Israel, but with Palestinian activists and with J Street" — a claim J Street publicly denied, with the group's president, Jeremy Ben-Ami, saying the organization "had nothing to do" with the visit. Leiter also suggested, without evidence, that Khanna's release of video footage was timed to distract from unrelated domestic political troubles.

Other pro-Israel figures accused Khanna of staging a "political stunt," while critics, including conservative commentator Tucker Carlson, argued the US ambassador to Israel should have defended an American lawmaker facing intimidation. Khanna has called for the arrest of the settlers involved and says he did notify Israeli authorities of his travel in advance.

The incident occurred against a backdrop of documented settler violence against American citizens in the West Bank: two US citizens have been killed in settler attacks in recent years with no suspects charged. Israel's West Bank occupation, including its settlements, was found unlawful by the International Court of Justice in 2024.
