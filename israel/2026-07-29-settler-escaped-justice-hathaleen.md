A year after Israeli settler Yinon Levi shot and killed Palestinian activist Awdah Hathaleen in the West Bank village of Umm al-Khair, a new investigation by the Palestinian rights group Al-Haq concludes he is still evading justice. Hathaleen, a 31-year-old father of three who had worked on production of the Oscar-winning documentary "No Other Land," was filming an armed settler raid on his village on 28 July 2025 when he was shot; Al-Haq's report, released on the killing's first anniversary and drawing on dozens of videos and photographs, legal testimony, and geospatial and open-source analysis, found that the single bullet which killed him matched the nine-millimetre handgun Levi was carrying.

Levi has claimed in Israeli court both that there was no correlation between his shot and Hathaleen's death and, separately, that he acted in self-defense. Al-Haq's report counters the self-defense claim directly, finding that none of the Palestinians present was armed and that Hathaleen posed no threat when Levi fired. The investigation also documents what it describes as an operational alignment between the settler and Israeli security forces: rather than being arrested at the scene, Levi was filmed standing alongside Israeli soldiers, pointing out Palestinians and international activists who were then taken into custody. When he was eventually detained, following international outcry, an Israeli court released him to just three days of house arrest.

The report further found that Israeli settlers blocked the access road for the ambulance responding to save Hathaleen's life while Israeli soldiers and police looked on, and that his body was withheld from his family for ten days before burial. Levi runs Meitarim Farm, an illegal outpost in the South Hebron Hills, and draws manpower from Hashomer Yosh, a settler group Al-Haq describes as part of a broader network propping up settlement expansion across the West Bank.

Al-Haq frames the killing not as an isolated act but as an example of intentional, state-backed violence. Separately, UN figures cited in the report show settler attacks have been on the rise in recent years. For Palestinians in villages like Umm al-Khair, mobile phone footage has become one of the only available tools to document such incidents and pursue accountability — a pursuit that, a year on, has yet to produce justice for Hathaleen's family.
