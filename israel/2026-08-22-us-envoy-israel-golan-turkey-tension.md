US Ambassador to Turkey and Special Envoy for Syria Tom Barrack has publicly broken with official US policy by saying Israel's occupation of Syria's Golan Heights continues in violation of UN resolutions and international law, despite President Trump's 2019 recognition of Israeli sovereignty over the territory. Barrack made the comments in a wide-ranging broadcast interview, saying "in the Golan with Syria, they still occupy the Golan against the UN resolutions, against all of the international order, which has said the Golan is Syria's." About 20,000 Israeli settlers currently live in the Golan alongside roughly 26,000 native, predominantly Druze inhabitants who identify as Syrian.

Barrack's remarks came alongside sharper criticism of an Israeli strike on Syria's Abu al-Duhur airbase, which he said "risked a direct confrontation with Turkey." He floated several explanations for the strike, ranging from faulty Israeli intelligence about Turkish military movements to a breakdown in coordination between Israel's military, Mossad and the prime minister's office. His "probably most likely" theory, he said, involved intercepted discussions about Turkey expanding its training presence at the base — though he also raised the more explosive possibility that "the Israelis were baiting the Turks," deliberately testing Ankara by flying aircraft toward its territory without prior notification.

Barrack said Washington's tolerance for continued Israeli military action was fraying, telling his interviewer that "America's support is waning. Even the young Democrats are coming against them." He said Trump had "drawn a line of attempting to go moderation" and that upcoming Israeli elections would reshape the political landscape further. On the broader question of Israeli intentions toward Lebanese and Syrian territory, he said a resolution ultimately depended on decisions by "Iran and Hezbollah," and warned that continued miscalculation carried real risk: "The unintended consequence of a mistake could be really consequential."

Barrack's public break with the administration's own recognition of Israeli sovereignty over the Golan — paired with his direct criticism of the Abu al-Duhur strike as needlessly risking a clash with a NATO ally — marks one of the sharpest rebukes of Israeli military conduct yet delivered by a sitting Trump administration official.
