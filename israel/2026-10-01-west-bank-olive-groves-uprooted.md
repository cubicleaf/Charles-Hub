Israeli forces are bulldozing Palestinian olive groves in several villages around Ramallah in the occupied West Bank, weeks before the October harvest, according to a Middle East Eye report published on 3 October 2026. Landowners and village officials describe a pattern of military orders demanding that trees and vegetation be removed, followed by bulldozers.

The Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission told the outlet that 55 military orders targeting trees and vegetation were issued between the start of the year and September, affecting about 2,373 dunams. By comparison, 47 orders covering 1,613 dunams were issued across all of 2025.

In Deir Nizam, the commission said an order was issued on 20 September, after a settler was killed in a shooting near the village, targeting about 99.79 dunams. A second order four days later gave landowners 12 hours to remove the trees themselves. Landowner Nathir Tamimi said about 140 dunams in the area had been cleared so far. Saleh Tamimi, standing among the uprooted trees at his home, said: "They want to displace us. I have nowhere else to go." He added that his house has also been issued a demolition order.

On 24 September, Israeli forces bulldozed about 31 dunams in Beit Rima, uprooting more than 400 olive trees two days after an order was issued. At Beit Ur al-Fuqa, bulldozing began on 25 September after a notice covering 29 dunams, which followed a car-ramming attack at a nearby checkpoint that injured an Israeli soldier; village council head Ali Samara said the clearing had extended to about 60 dunams and warned that at least 1,500 trees, some more than 200 years old, were at risk. A separate order in Kafr Malik covers more than 56 dunams.

Farmers quoted say the loss goes beyond this season, because uprooted trees can take years to become productive again.
