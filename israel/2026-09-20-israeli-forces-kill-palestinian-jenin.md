Israeli forces shot and killed a Palestinian man near the city of Jenin in the northern occupied West Bank on 20 September 2026 after he allegedly "attempted to carry out a vehicular attack" near the illegal settlement of Ganim, an army spokesperson said, adding there were no Israeli casualties. The Palestine Red Crescent Society said Israeli forces prevented its medical team from reaching the victim, according to the Wafa news agency. In a separate incident the same day, an Israeli settler was fatally shot near the illegal settlement of Halamish in the central West Bank; the 30-year-old man later died of his wounds in hospital. Israeli forces said the shooting suspect fled on foot after opening fire from a vehicle and was later detained, wounded, at a hospital where he had sought treatment.

National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir demanded the suspect in the Halamish shooting face execution, writing on X that it was the "first case that meets the criteria of the Death Penalty Law" since the Knesset adopted the measure in March 2026. The law allows military courts to sentence Palestinians convicted of killing Israelis to death but does not apply the same penalty to Israelis convicted of killing Palestinians. Ben-Gvir had been a strident campaigner for the law's passage. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said he had ordered the military and security agencies to increase their forces and carry out arrests and offensive operations in the area, and ordered the demolition of the suspect's family home. Following the shooting, Israeli forces closed all entry and exit points to the cities of Ramallah and el-Bireh.

The incidents come amid a sharp escalation in West Bank violence since Israel's war in Gaza began in October 2023. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs recorded more than 1,330 settler-related attacks on Palestinians between January and late July 2026 alone, averaging six a day and marking the fastest pace of such attacks in roughly 20 years. Rights groups say settlers, often operating alongside or protected by Israeli soldiers, are increasingly armed with military-issued weaponry and wear military uniforms during raids, blurring the line between settler and soldier in attacks researchers have described as pogroms.
