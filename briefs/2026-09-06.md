## 1. Claude Formalizes Fermat's Last Theorem
**Anthropic · Sept 4 · 2 days ago**

Claude produced a complete, computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean proof language, finishing August 18 after 11 days of largely autonomous work on Prove2Me, a formalization platform built by Columbia University's Tianyi Peng. The run generated over 13 million lines of Lean code — more than five times the size of Lean's standard math library — proving 30,300 intermediate theorems on roughly 6 billion output tokens from a model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1. Reviewer Kevin Buzzard of Imperial College London called it an "extraordinary autoformalization achievement."

*Why it matters:* it's the largest machine-verified proof ever produced, and Anthropic says it needed only sparse high-level human steering — a concrete data point on how far agentic reasoning has moved into domains once considered exclusively human.

## 2. NHTSA Opens a Formal Audit Into Tesla's Cybercab
**Tesla / NHTSA · Sept 3-4 · Still developing**

NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation opened audit query AQ26002 on September 3, covering roughly 1,000 steering-wheel-free Cybercabs Tesla put into paid service in Austin, to examine how Tesla self-certified a vehicle without manual controls against safety standards written around them. Tesla shares fell 6% Friday as Wells Fargo flagged "early execution issues," including rider complaints about routing errors and long waits.

*Why it matters:* yesterday's regulatory question now has a case number, and the fleet's real-world performance problems are surfacing before the audit even concludes.

## 3. Google, OpenAI and Anthropic All Ship Cyberattack-Capable AI
**Google / OpenAI / Anthropic · Sept 1-2 · Still developing**

Within days of each other, Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, which it says beats Anthropic's Mythos 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol at autonomous vulnerability discovery; OpenAI confirmed its forthcoming Astra model crosses its Preparedness Framework's "Critical" cyber threshold after chaining two zero-days into a working browser-and-OS exploit; and Anthropic rolled out Enterprise Frontier Safeguards alongside a restricted-access Mythos 5.1. All three are gating the most capable versions behind vetted-defender programs — Google's Fairwind, OpenAI's Daybreak Blue.

*Why it matters:* frontier labs are now building models explicitly for offensive cyber capability and racing to build the access controls around them at the same time, not after.

## 4. Microsoft's MAI-Transcribe-2 Undercuts Rivals on Price and Speed
**Microsoft AI · Sept 3 · 3 days ago**

Microsoft released MAI-Transcribe-2, a speech-to-text model covering 60 languages, priced at $0.10 per audio hour through the end of 2026 — about 72% below its predecessor. Microsoft says it's up to 10x faster than OpenAI's GPT-Transcribe and 5x faster than Google's Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, while topping the FLEURS benchmark with a 5.2% average word-error rate.

*Why it matters:* transcription is turning into a commodity price war, and every product built on top of it — call centers, meeting notes, captioning — inherits the new economics.

## 5. PsiQuantum and Brookhaven Lab Target Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing by 2028
**PsiQuantum / Brookhaven National Laboratory · Sept 2 · 4 days ago**

PsiQuantum and Brookhaven National Laboratory announced a partnership under which Brookhaven scientists will use PsiQuantum's Construct software to design and optimize utility-scale quantum workloads, directly supporting the Department of Energy's Quantum Genesis initiative, which targets a fault-tolerant, scientifically useful quantum computer by 2028.

*Why it matters:* a national lab now has a concrete software pipeline aimed at a specific fault-tolerance deadline, rather than an open-ended research timeline.
