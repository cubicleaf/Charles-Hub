*Today's throughline: AI's center of gravity is splitting geopolitically, financially, and technically — while the humans it's automating start pushing back.*

## 1. China launches a global AI governance body without the West
**WAICO · Shanghai · July 17 · 2 days ago**

Twenty-nine countries signed the founding charter of the World AI Cooperation Organization on July 16, with UN Secretary-General António Guterres attending; Xi Jinping opened the World AI Conference the next day, calling AI development "a symphony of international cooperation," not "a solo performance by a single country." Founders include Brazil, Indonesia, Russia, Pakistan, Malaysia, and South Africa — the US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, and Australia all stayed out.

*Why it matters:* There are now two competing blueprints for who writes AI's rules, and most of the Global South just picked a side.

## 2. Apple overtakes Nvidia as the most valuable company on Earth
**Apple / Nvidia · July 17 · 2 days ago**

Apple closed Friday at a $4.88 trillion market cap, edging past Nvidia's roughly $4.86 trillion after Nvidia shares dropped about 3.5% — Apple's first time back on top since April 2025, as investors rotate toward the company monetizing AI through devices already in a billion pockets.

*Why it matters:* Money is betting AI's winners will be distribution plays, not just infrastructure.

## 3. Google delays Gemini 3.5 Pro again, Alphabet sheds $200 billion
**Google · July 16 · 3 days ago**

Gemini 3.5 Pro, promised for June after its May unveiling, still hasn't shipped — its coding and reasoning performance fell short internally. Alphabet shares fell about 4.4% on the report, wiping out roughly $200 billion in a session, more than the company's projected 2026 capital budget.

*Why it matters:* A second slip on Google's flagship model signals it's losing the coding-model race to OpenAI and open-weight rivals.

## 4. Hyundai workers strike again over the Atlas humanoid robot
**Hyundai Motor · Ulsan, South Korea · July 13-15 · Still developing**

Hyundai's union walked off shifts early for three days after 15 rounds of failed wage talks, with four-hour stoppages now set for July 20-22 — the auto industry's first strike aimed at humanoid deployment. Workers want hourly pay converted to fixed salaries and retirement age raised from 60 to 65 before Boston Dynamics' 190cm Atlas robot reaches the line, which the union says can't happen without its consent.

*Why it matters:* This is the first real-world test of labor negotiating against humanoid automation.

## 5. Kimi K3 becomes the largest open-weight model, and a top coder
**Moonshot AI · July 16 · 3 days ago**

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, scored 88.3 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — second globally behind only OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol, the first open-weight model to crack that benchmark's top two. Full weights ship July 27.

*Why it matters:* If a free, self-hostable model keeps beating paid frontier models at coding, subscription economics get harder to defend.
