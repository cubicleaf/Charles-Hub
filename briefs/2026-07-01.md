*The last 48 hours are less about new capability than about who controls access to it — Washington reversed one AI export restriction while actively enforcing another.*

## 1. US lifts export controls on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5
**Anthropic / Dept. of Commerce · July 1 · 0 days ago**

Commerce lifted the export controls it imposed June 12, which had cut off all foreign nationals — including Anthropic's own foreign-national staff — from Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 over national-security concerns. The administration says it's satisfied Anthropic addressed the underlying risks; Anthropic is restoring access now.

*Why it matters:* This is the first walk-back in the White House's emerging practice of gating frontier AI releases directly — a precedent other labs will invoke next time they're targeted.

## 2. Anthropic launches Claude Sonnet 5
**Anthropic · June 30 · 1 day ago**

Sonnet 5 is now the default model on Free and Pro plans, priced at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens through August 31 (then $3/$15). Anthropic says it closes most of the agentic-coding and computer-use gap with Opus 4.8, and beats it on knowledge work, while being the most autonomous Sonnet-tier model yet at tool use and multi-step planning.

*Why it matters:* A cheaper model that narrows the gap to the flagship changes the cost math for teams currently defaulting to a pricier tier for agentic work.

## 3. GPT-5.6 launches restricted to ~20 government-approved partners
**OpenAI · ~June 26 · Still developing**

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, Luna) advances software engineering, computer use, and cybersecurity benchmarks, with a new "ultra" mode that spins up subagents for complex work. At the Trump administration's request, it ships only to roughly 20 vetted government and enterprise partners, not the open market.

*Why it matters:* Government-gated access, not compute or safety limits, is becoming the real bottleneck on frontier deployment.

## 4. Samsung and SK Hynix commit $516B to new memory fabs
**South Korea · June 30 · 1 day ago**

Samsung and SK Hynix will each build two new fabs, part of an ₩800 trillion ($516B) buildout backed by Seoul to expand memory and HBM capacity. SK Hynix alone committed $7.9B to ASML for EUV lithography tools through 2027. ASML hit a record on the news; Applied Materials and KLA both rose more than 5%.

*Why it matters:* This is capacity bought today for chips that won't ship for 18+ months — a bet that AI memory demand keeps compounding past 2027.

## 5. Qualcomm buys AI-software startup Modular for $3.9B
**Qualcomm · June 24 · Still developing**

The all-stock deal gives Qualcomm the compiler and runtime software Modular built to run AI models across chip architectures without Nvidia's CUDA lock-in. Qualcomm paired it with a new Dragonfly data-center CPU line and raised its FY2029 non-handset revenue target to $40B.

*Why it matters:* Clearest sign yet that Nvidia's real moat is software, not silicon — and rivals are now buying their way around it instead of out-engineering it.

## Under the radar
SpaceX's Colossus 2 data center in Memphis starts collecting $150M/month from AI startup Reflection today, part of a deal that could total $6.3B through 2029 for Nvidia GB300 access — a reminder of how much AI compute financing now runs through non-cloud players. Separately, Amazon's Zoox unveiled its production-intent robotaxi with ergonomic seating and a first-responder communication interface, ahead of a commercial launch later this year.

*Why it matters:* Both show AI infrastructure and robotics commercialization moving from pilot to production money, outside the usual OpenAI/Anthropic/Google cycle.
