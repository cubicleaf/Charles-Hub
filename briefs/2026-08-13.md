*Today's theme: AI is now fast enough to break critical software in a day, and to justify half-trillion-dollar bets on its own compute.*

## 1. AI finds a zero-click Zoom flaw in a single day
**Ⓐ Security / Zoom · Aug 11, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Researchers used fewer than 20 prompts on public AI models to find and weaponize a critical Zoom flaw in under 24 hours: CVE-2026-53413 ("Zoomsday"), plus two related bugs. A missing bounds check in Zoom's annotation feature let any meeting participant run code on another attendee's device with zero clicks, on every platform Zoom ships. Zoom patched it in June and July, ahead of the public write-up Aug. 11; Zoom serves 70% of the Fortune 100.

*Why it matters:* Finding a nation-state-grade vulnerability just collapsed from a specialized team's project to one afternoon.

## 2. Nvidia lines up over $500B from Wall Street for AI compute
**Nvidia · Aug 10, 2026 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

Nvidia signed MOUs with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR to build platforms aimed at mobilizing over $500 billion in third-party capital for AI compute. The deals are non-binding; CEO Jensen Huang said none of the six firms he approached turned him down, and Apollo's president called compute "a scarce, mission-critical asset class." The money flows to Nvidia's customers, not Nvidia itself.

*Why it matters:* Wall Street underwriting GPU capacity like real estate confirms AI compute is now its own asset class.

## 3. Lovable doubles its valuation to $13.3B in eight months
**Lovable · Aug 12, 2026 · 1 day ago**

The Stockholm "vibe-coding" startup closed a $400 million Series C led by Menlo Ventures and the Scaleup Europe Fund, roughly doubling December's $6.6 billion valuation. Lovable, which builds software from natural-language prompts, has hosted 60 million-plus projects and says it's running at roughly $500 million in annualized revenue.

*Why it matters:* Natural-language app-building is one of the few AI categories converting usage into fast-growing revenue, not just hype.

## 4. Google ships Pixel 11 and a new Tensor G6 chip
**Google · Aug 12, 2026 · 1 day ago**

At its "Made by Google" event, Google launched four phones — Pixel 11 ($899) through Pixel 11 Pro Fold ($1,899) — plus the Pixel Watch 5 and a new tracker, Pixel Tag. All run the Tensor G6 chip, which Google says delivers 50% more on-device AI compute and 20% better power efficiency than its predecessor. Preorders opened Aug. 12; availability follows Aug. 20.

*Why it matters:* Google is betting its own silicon roadmap, not just its models, on running more AI on-device.

## 5. A March breach of an AI proxy library hit 2,500+ firms
**CloudSEK · Aug 11, 2026 · 2 days ago**

A new CloudSEK report details how attackers compromised the Trivy scanner inside LiteLLM's build pipeline, publishing poisoned PyPI packages live about 40 minutes in March. CloudSEK estimates 2,500+ companies and 434,000 CI/CD pipelines were exposed, with high-confidence matches including AWS, Cisco, Salesforce, Siemens, Airbus, and X Corp; stolen assets reportedly include cloud keys, SSH keys, and AI provider API keys.

*Why it matters:* A 40-minute compromise of one library rippled into hundreds of enterprise supply chains, and took months to measure.
