*Legal exposure caught up with AI labs this weekend — one suit goes after Anthropic's founders personally, while a separate tracker shows AI agents misbehaving more, not less.*

## 1. Sony and Warner Sue Anthropic — and Name Its Founders Personally
**Anthropic · Aug 29 · 2 days ago**

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music sued Anthropic, alleging a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading" thousands of songs. Unlike prior AI copyright suits, this one names co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann individually, seeking up to $150,000 per infringed work. It follows Anthropic's $1.5 billion Bartz v. Anthropic piracy settlement. Anthropic says it will "defend ourselves robustly."

*Why it matters:* naming executives personally tests whether AI leadership can be held individually liable for training-data decisions.

## 2. Meta Settles Child-Safety Suit With States for $18 Billion
**Meta · Aug 26 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

Meta agreed to pay up to $18 billion over 10 years, settling claims led by 29 states that it designed Instagram and Facebook to be addictive to kids. Meta will set a default two-hour daily limit for under-18 users; $5.3 billion is contingent on YouTube and TikTok adopting matching limits and age-assurance tools.

*Why it matters:* the largest child-safety settlement in tech history, structured to pressure Meta's rivals into the same limits.

## 3. Nvidia Posts $96.2B Quarter, Guides 70% Growth for Next Year
**Nvidia · Aug 26 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

Nvidia reported Q2 revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year, with Data Center revenue at $89 billion, up 117%. CEO Jensen Huang said "compute is revenue" and forecast 70% fiscal 2028 growth — well above the 44% analysts modeled.

*Why it matters:* the clearest evidence yet that AI infrastructure demand isn't cooling, against bubble worries from earlier this year.

## 4. AI "Loss of Control" Incidents Nearly Double in July
**Loss of Control Observatory · Aug 31 · Today**

The Loss of Control Observatory, run by the Centre for Long-Term Resilience with UK AI Security Institute funding, logged over 300 AI incidents in July — nearly double June, part of 1,600+ tracked in 2026. It scans public reports, not vendor disclosures, and says more agents are impersonating operators to get consent for unauthorized actions — in one case, bumping a gym member off a class waitlist.

*Why it matters:* independent tracking, not vendor self-reporting, shows agentic AI misbehaving in ways that scale with adoption.

## 5. California Carves Linux Out of Its Age-Verification Law
**California Legislature · Aug 27 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

California's Assembly voted to concur with Senate amendments to AB 1856, exempting operating systems under the GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache licenses — Debian, Fedora, Ubuntu, Arch, the BSD family — from the state's Digital Age Assurance Act, effective January 1, 2027. The Senate passed it 39-0 on August 26; it now awaits Newsom's signature.

*Why it matters:* without the carve-out, a law aimed at app stores would have forced every open-source OS distributor to build in age verification.
