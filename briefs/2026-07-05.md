*The AI industry's entanglement with government is no longer theoretical — it's showing up in equity terms, court filings, and export-control workarounds all at once.*

## 1. OpenAI offers the US government a 5% stake
**OpenAI · July 2 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

The Financial Times reported OpenAI is discussing giving the US government a 5% stake, worth roughly $42.6 billion at its $852 billion valuation, ahead of an IPO. The proposal, floated to Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Bessent, would seed a fund modeled on the Alaska Permanent Fund. Anthropic is resisting, pushing an AI-sector tax instead.

*Why it matters:* A direct government stake in a frontier lab would be unprecedented, and would pressure every other lab to match it or explain why not.

## 2. Unsealed emails complicate the Pentagon's case against Anthropic
**US District Court, N.D. Cal. · July 4 · 1 day ago**

Court filings unsealed this week show Pentagon official Emil Michael told Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei the two sides were "very close" on contract terms — a day after the Pentagon finalized a supply-chain risk designation against Anthropic, before the company was notified. The dispute centers on Anthropic's refusal to drop bans on fully autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance from Claude's usage terms. The judge called the contradiction "exceedingly difficult to square" with the government's "hostile" characterization of the company.

*Why it matters:* A national-security rationale applied selectively against a company still negotiating in good faith — a precedent that matters for any AI vendor selling into defense.

## 3. Anthropic explores a custom AI chip with Samsung
**The Information / TechCrunch · July 2 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

Anthropic has held early talks with Samsung about manufacturing a custom AI chip on its 2nm process, following OpenAI and Google into in-house silicon. The project is exploratory — no specs or timeline yet — but Anthropic has hired silicon engineers, and Samsung invested in Anthropic's recent $65 billion raise.

*Why it matters:* Every major lab betting on custom silicon is signaling the same conclusion: renting Nvidia GPUs at current prices doesn't scale to where demand is going.

## 4. Meituan open-sources a 1.6T model trained entirely on Chinese chips
**Meituan · June 30 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

Meituan released LongCat-2.0, a 1.6-trillion-parameter, MIT-licensed model trained from scratch on 50,000 domestic Chinese AI chips rather than Nvidia GPUs. It's already near the top of OpenRouter's coding-usage rankings.

*Why it matters:* Clear evidence that US export controls haven't stopped China from pretraining frontier-scale models — only from doing it on Nvidia hardware.

## 5. Realta Fusion converts plasma energy directly into electricity
**Realta Fusion, Madison, WI · June 30 · 5 days ago**

Realta Fusion became the first commercial fusion company to demonstrate direct energy conversion, drawing multiple amps at roughly 100 volts from its WHAM reactor — enough to light several bulbs. It's not net electricity generation, which awaits future reactors, but it validates a path that skips the steam turbine entirely.

*Why it matters:* Direct conversion, if it scales, could make fusion plants cheaper and simpler than the turbine-based designs everyone else depends on.
