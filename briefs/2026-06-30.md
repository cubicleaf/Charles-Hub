*Governments are setting the terms for which AI models exist; markets are setting the terms for who can afford them.*

## 1. US Partially Lifts Anthropic Mythos Export Block
**Anthropic / US Commerce Dept. · June 26–27 · 3 days ago**

After suspending Mythos 5 on June 12 under an emergency export control directive—citing fears the model could be weaponized to find and exploit software vulnerabilities at unprecedented speed—Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick authorized a partial restoration on June 26. Roughly 100 vetted US companies and federal agencies now have access under safeguards Anthropic agreed to but has not disclosed; Fable 5, the less capable sibling, remains blocked. The two-week standoff, described by multiple outlets as the most consequential legal confrontation between an AI lab and the US government in history, ended with Anthropic in a substantially weaker negotiating position than it started.

*Why it matters:* This is the first time the US government has used emergency export controls to suspend a commercially-deployed frontier AI model and then selectively reissued access—establishing a template for how Washington can throttle AI distribution without legislation or a full ban.

## 2. GitHub Copilot's Token-Billing First Close Confirms Cost Shock
**GitHub (Microsoft) · June 29–30 · Today · Still developing**

Today is the first full monthly billing close since GitHub switched Copilot from flat-rate to token-based pricing on June 1. Developers have been sharing screenshots since launch day: $29/month Pro accounts are projecting $750 bills; Pro+ users running agentic PR automation and multi-file refactors are hitting $3,000+. One developer on the $39 Pro+ plan burned 8% of their monthly AI-credit allotment in two hours. Inline autocomplete remains unlimited; pain concentrates on agentic and multi-turn workflows, and community reaction on Reddit and X has been immediate and vocal.

*Why it matters:* The new cost structure makes Copilot comparable to or more expensive than dedicated coding-agent subscriptions for high-use teams, accelerating enterprise evaluation of alternatives including Cursor, Windsurf, and Claude Code.

## 3. Google DeepMind Loses Six Researchers in Thirty Days
**Google DeepMind · June 18–28 · Still developing**

Noam Shazeer—co-author of "Attention Is All You Need" and the subject of a $2.7 billion acqui-hire from Character.ai in 2024—announced his move to OpenAI on June 18. Nobel chemistry laureate John Jumper (AlphaFold lead) followed the next day, joining Anthropic. Senior researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel (AlphaFold and Gemini contributors) also defected to Anthropic. Denny Zhou, who built Google Brain's multi-step reasoning research team, had already been at Meta for four months. The Shazeer and Jumper announcements alone triggered a single-day ~5% drop in Alphabet shares, erasing roughly $225 billion in market cap.

*Why it matters:* Anthropic and OpenAI are absorbing the researchers most responsible for the foundational architecture (Shazeer), structural biology AI (Jumper), and multi-step reasoning (Zhou) that made Google's AI credible at the research frontier—a skills transfer with compounding effects over the next model generation.

## 4. Qualcomm in Advanced Talks to Acquire Tenstorrent for $8–10B
**Qualcomm / Tenstorrent · June 16 · 2 weeks ago · Still developing**

Qualcomm is negotiating to acquire Tenstorrent, the RISC-V AI chip startup co-founded by Jim Keller, in a deal valued between $8 billion and $10 billion. Intel was also reportedly interested as recently as May, introducing competitive bidding dynamics. A closed deal would give Qualcomm a data-center AI silicon presence it currently lacks; Tenstorrent's Wormhole and Blackhole chips are designed as open-ISA alternatives to NVIDIA's CUDA-locked ecosystem. The startup was valued at $2.6 billion in its December 2024 Series D.

*Why it matters:* A Qualcomm-Tenstorrent combination is the most credible threat yet to NVIDIA's data-center GPU monopoly—not because RISC-V outperforms Hopper today, but because it gives hyperscalers a second supplier they can actually negotiate with on architecture and licensing terms.

## 5. Microsoft Majorana 2: 1,000x Qubit Stability, Roadmap Cut to 2029
**Microsoft · June 2–3 · 4 weeks ago · Still developing**

Announced at Microsoft Build 2026, Majorana 2 replaces aluminum with lead in its superconducting material stack—a change that shields topological qubits from cosmic-ray interference and extends mean qubit lifetime from milliseconds (typical for competing approaches) to 20 seconds, with instances exceeding one minute. The chip scales to 12 qubits, up from 8 in Majorana 1. Microsoft now targets a fault-tolerant, scalable quantum computer by 2029, halving its prior roadmap, and credits its Microsoft Discovery agentic AI platform with accelerating the materials science iteration. Independent physicists note the preprint lacks repeatable multi-device data.

*Why it matters:* If the qubit lifetimes hold at scale, Majorana 2 leapfrogs the error-correction challenge stalling every competing quantum program—but the absence of reproducible external validation keeps this in "extraordinary claim" territory for now.

## Under the radar

**White House AI Innovation and Security Executive Order · June 2 · Still developing**
Trump signed an EO on June 2 establishing a voluntary federal framework for frontier-model review: developers submit models to government red-teamers for up to 30 days before public release, receiving liability protections in exchange. The order also creates an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse for sharing vulnerability data across critical infrastructure and explicitly bars mandatory licensing. The Mythos restoration negotiation (story #1) operated under this exact framework—making the EO the quiet legal scaffolding behind June's biggest AI governance story.

*Why it matters:* The 30-day voluntary window gives the government a legitimate on-ramp to gate dangerous models without legislation, and agencies including DoD and DHS are already citing it in AI procurement guidance.
