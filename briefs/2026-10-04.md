## 1. GSA writes LLM data limits into federal contracts
**GSA · Oct 2 · 2 days ago**

The General Services Administration added a clause, "Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems," effective October 19. Contractors cannot use government data to train LLMs, inform advertising, or sell it to third parties. They must disclose LLM use within 120 days, report incidents within 72 hours, delete embeddings and fine-tuned weights at contract closeout, and give 30 days' notice before major model updates. Back-office systems and incidental LLM functionality are exempt, and GSA dropped the "unbiased AI principles" language industry called untestable.

*Why it matters:* Weight and embedding deletion at closeout is a concrete technical obligation that vendors will have to engineer for.

## 2. Claude Sonnet 5.5 scores above Opus 5.5 on a coding benchmark
**Anthropic · Sep 28 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 runs more than 30% faster than Sonnet 5 and costs up to 30% less per task, at the same $2/$10 per million input/output tokens. It scores 70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, against 10.3% for Sonnet 5, and 80.1% on OSWorld 2.1. Anthropic says a cheaper Haiku 5.5 is coming. These are vendor-reported numbers.

*Why it matters:* If the scores hold up independently, the mid tier covers work that previously needed the flagship.

## 3. Foldable iPhone yields lag ahead of Apple's home-hardware event
**Apple · Oct 3 · 1 day ago**

MacRumors reports Foxconn-sourced final assembly yields on the foldable "only slightly above 60% as of September 17," with maturity possibly six months to a year away. Apple will introduce a smart home hub, a new HomePod mini and updated Apple TV models on October 13. The hub has a square 6-inch display and a FaceTime camera. Separately, Apple confirmed that some iPhone 18 Pro Max units on AT&T lose cellular service and need replacement.

*Why it matters:* Low foldable yields point to constrained supply, while the October 13 event is Apple's first real home-platform push in years.

## 4. Gemini 4 Argon goes to defenders first
**Google · Sep 30 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

Google says Argon scores 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, 51.3% on AutomationBench, and ties for first at 68% on CWE-bench v1 for vulnerability remediation. It carries a 1 million token output limit, up from 64K. Access starts through the Fairwind Program for trusted cyber defenders, with introductory pricing of $2 and $10 per million tokens. Broader release follows phased testing and U.S. government voluntary pre-release review.

*Why it matters:* Gating a frontier model by who is using it, not just by what it refuses, is becoming a release pattern.

## 5. OpenAI shelves Astra 6.1, ships Dots
**OpenAI · Sep 28-29 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

OpenAI halted GPT-6.1 Astra after its safety head said it "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization." A day later at DevDay it launched Dots, always-on agents running on GPT-6 Astra, plus GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says delivers near-Astra intelligence at a fifth of Astra's token prices. A new Pro 500 tier costs $500 a month, and Dots are excluded in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK at launch.

*Why it matters:* The scope-and-authorization failure is the exact risk always-on agents amplify.
