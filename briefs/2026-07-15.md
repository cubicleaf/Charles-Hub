*Regulators on two continents moved against AI's physical and social footprint this week, even as Wall Street doubled down on the chips and companies building it.*

## 1. New York imposes the nation's first data center moratorium
**Gov. Kathy Hochul · July 14 · 1 day ago**

Hochul signed an executive order pausing state environmental permits for new hyperscale data centers for up to a year — the first statewide moratorium of its kind. The DEC will withhold discretionary permits while regulators draft an environmental impact statement; Hochul cited AI-driven strain on utility bills, water supplies, and the grid.

*Why it matters:* New York just handed every other state a template for slowing hyperscaler buildout, right as AI capex tops U.S. corporate spending.

## 2. China's anthropomorphic-AI rules take effect, and Doubao and Qwen go dark
**Cyberspace Administration of China · Effective July 15 · Today**

Beijing's Interim Measures for AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Services took effect today, requiring anti-addiction systems and instant-exit mechanisms for AI built to sustain emotional engagement. Rather than retrofit, ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen are killing their custom-agent features outright: personas stop working, and Doubao's data becomes unrecoverable after October 15.

*Why it matters:* It's the first national law targeting companion-style AI, and two of China's biggest AI products chose to shut a feature down rather than build around it.

## 3. TSMC posts an all-time revenue record as N3 sells out
**TSMC · Reported July 13 · 2 days ago**

June revenue hit NT$442.68 billion, up roughly 68% year-over-year, breaking a four-year seasonal slump and beating its own guidance. Its N3 node and CoWoS packaging — behind nearly every leading AI GPU and CPU this year — are booked solid through year-end.

*Why it matters:* When the industry's sole advanced-node fab is sold out for six months, every AI hardware roadmap downstream is capacity-constrained, not compute-constrained.

## 4. Goldman Sachs tells clients to go long China's AI stack
**Goldman Sachs · Published July 9 · Still developing**

Goldman's thematic-investing desk added China's AI sector to its core recommendations, telling clients to rotate out of South Korean AI assets and into Chinese ones, naming Zhipu, DeepSeek, and ByteDance as preferred plays. It argues Chinese open-weight models have closed the gap to frontier U.S. systems at a fraction of the parameter count and cost, and projects Chinese AI subscription revenue growing roughly 25-fold by 2030.

*Why it matters:* A top-tier Wall Street bank is now formally pricing Chinese open-weight AI as investable, not merely competitive on benchmarks.

## 5. China's humanoid robot makers scramble to go public
**Unitree, LimX Dynamics · This week · Still developing**

Unitree cleared its final regulatory hurdle for a Shanghai listing, aiming to raise roughly $618 million at a valuation near 42 billion yuan, with a debut possible as soon as late July. Rival LimX Dynamics just closed a pre-IPO round at a $2.2 billion valuation, as over 100 Chinese humanoid startups chase capital.

*Why it matters:* China's humanoid race is shifting from demo videos to public-market discipline, which will start exposing which companies actually have revenue.

## Under the radar
**Amazon is closing Mechanical Turk to new customers on July 30**, ending two decades of the crowdsourced-task marketplace that helped train early machine learning — partly because many of its "human" workers were found quietly using LLMs to do the work.

**OpenAI's pitch to give the U.S. government a roughly $42.6 billion, 5% stake is still unresolved.** Altman wants Anthropic, Google, and Meta to match it; none has agreed.
