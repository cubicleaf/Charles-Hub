*Chip supply and AI governance both hit inflection points this week: memory makers are racing to Wall Street to fund capacity, while governments and the labs themselves start negotiating who actually controls the technology.*

## 1. SK Hynix files for $29.4B Nasdaq IPO
**SK Hynix · July 5, 2026 · 1 day ago**

SK Hynix filed to dual-list on Nasdaq as SKHY, targeting 45.45 trillion won ($29.4B), with trading starting July 10. The company holds over half the global HBM market and is Nvidia's primary memory partner; proceeds fund new Korean fabs and EUV scanners due by December 2027.

*Why it matters:* the clearest bet yet that AI accelerator demand has years of capex left to fund, not just model hype.

## 2. OpenAI floats 5% government stake
**OpenAI · July 2, 2026 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

Per the FT, Sam Altman raised giving Washington a 5% OpenAI stake (~$42.6B at its $852B valuation) with Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Bessent, alongside a pitch for an Alaska Permanent Fund-style vehicle funded by every major US AI lab. Talks are early and would likely need congressional action.

*Why it matters:* a government equity stake in a frontier AI developer puts the state in an unresolved conflict as both regulator and shareholder.

## 3. UN opens first Global Dialogue on AI Governance
**United Nations · July 6, 2026 · today**

All 193 UN member states, plus industry and civil society, convened in Geneva for a two-day session on international AI governance, running alongside the WSIS Forum and ahead of the ITU's AI for Good Summit. It's the first UN venue built specifically for cross-government AI coordination; a second session follows in New York in May 2027.

*Why it matters:* the international counterweight to deals like OpenAI's — enforceable norms or just a communiqué will shape how fragmented AI regulation gets.

## 4. Tesla caps employee AI spending at $200/week
**Tesla · July 6, 2026 · today**

Tesla's new policy requires manager sign-off for employees exceeding $200 a week in AI tool spending, after engineers were burning thousands of dollars in tokens weekly. The cap exempts xAI's Grok betas — though by internal accounts, Tesla engineers actually prefer Claude. Uber, Meta, Amazon, and Walmart have imposed similar caps this year.

*Why it matters:* token billing is making AI's marginal cost visible to employees, and companies are treating it like any other budget line.

## 5. Micron commits $9.3B to Japan HBM expansion
**Micron · July 5, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Micron is expanding its Hiroshima plant to produce next-gen high-bandwidth memory, backed by $3.1B in subsidies from Japan's trade ministry. Shipments start around summer 2028, making Micron a third major HBM supplier alongside SK Hynix and Samsung.

*Why it matters:* three-way HBM competition, instead of a Samsung/SK Hynix duopoly, could ease the memory bottleneck on AI accelerators.

## Under the radar
**Midjourney · July 4, 2026 · 2 days ago.** Midjourney is asking a federal judge to force Disney, Universal, and Warner Bros. to disclose their internal generative AI tools, training data, and board presentations — arguing the studios suing it for copyright infringement may do the same thing internally. *Why it matters:* discovery here could be the first real public look at how studios actually use AI in production.

III shareholders vote today on the SPAC merger taking General Fusion public on Nasdaq as GFUZ, after its LM26 reactor hit 0.72 keV in plasma-heating tests. *Why it matters:* it tests investor appetite for pre-commercial fusion as a public asset class.
