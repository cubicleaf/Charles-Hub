## 1. Pentagon Adds ChatGPT and Grok to Its AI Portal — Still Without Claude
**Department of Defense · Aug 31 · Yesterday**

The Pentagon added custom versions of ChatGPT ("ChatGPT Mil") and xAI's "Grok for Government" to GenAI.mil, its secure portal for commercial AI models, alongside the Google Gemini access it launched with last year. The portal has onboarded over 1.7 million of the department's 3 million personnel. Anthropic's Claude is absent: the DoD has been in an ongoing dispute with Anthropic after the company refused "unrestricted use" and insisted on safety guardrails, and the Trump administration separately labeled Anthropic a supply-chain risk. A federal judge ruled that designation illegal on Thursday, calling it retaliation against a government critic, but the Pentagon is reportedly continuing to migrate off Claude regardless, with the government expected to appeal.

*Why it matters:* winning in court doesn't guarantee an AI lab keeps its biggest institutional customer if it won't drop safety conditions.

## 2. Tim Cook Steps Down as Apple CEO After 15 Years
**Apple · Sep 1 · Today**

Tim Cook moved into the role of Executive Chairman effective today, handing the CEO job to John Ternus, 50, Apple's hardware engineering chief since 2021 and an Apple employee since 2001. The board approved the succession unanimously; Cook's 15-year run ends as one of the most successful tenures in tech history. Board chair Art Levinson becomes Lead Independent Director on the same date.

*Why it matters:* the world's most valuable company now runs on a hardware engineer's judgment rather than Cook's operations-and-supply-chain playbook.

## 3. OpenAI Cuts Off SpaceX-Owned Cursor's Model Access
**OpenAI · Aug 29 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

OpenAI said it will end Cursor's access to its models on Nov. 12, after SpaceX closed its $60 billion acquisition of the coding startup on Aug. 14. OpenAI said it can't be confident SpaceX will honor its usage terms, citing Musk's own admission in court that xAI trained on OpenAI's outputs. The practical hit is small — OpenAI models make up about 5% of Cursor's traffic — and Anthropic said it would expand compute for Claude inside Cursor instead.

*Why it matters:* a frontier lab cutting off a customer over its new owner, not its behavior, sets a precedent other labs may follow as AI-native tools keep getting acquired.

## 4. China-Linked Hackers Turn Cisco Routers Into Spy Platforms
**Sygnia · Aug 31 · Yesterday**

Sygnia disclosed that Fire Ant, a China-nexus group previously known for hijacking VMware hypervisors, has expanded into Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS authentication servers, and Linux management hosts. The group compromised a TACACS server to intercept credentials and used a novel implant, dubbed BridgeAgent, disguised as Zabbix monitoring software to tunnel traffic while hiding its tracks from admins running standard diagnostic commands. Sygnia says the group used its foothold to probe — but hasn't confirmed breaching — connected critical-infrastructure networks.

*Why it matters:* the campaign lives inside the routing and authentication layer other defenses assume is trustworthy, not in an endpoint antivirus catches.

## 5. SK Hynix Breaks Ground on $4 Billion Indiana HBM Plant
**SK hynix · Aug 28 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

SK hynix held a groundbreaking ceremony for a packaging facility in Indiana dedicated to high-bandwidth memory, the chip type that feeds AI accelerators. The plant targets a cleanroom by October 2028 and mass production of next-generation HBM in the second half of 2029, with roughly 1,000 workers once running. Indiana's governor and Sen. Todd Young attended alongside South Korean officials.

*Why it matters:* HBM remains the tightest bottleneck in AI chip supply, and this is one of the few fabs on a public timeline to ease it.
