## 1. Hugging Face's board is fielding a $13 billion buyout
**Hugging Face · Aug 24, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Hugging Face is in early talks to be acquired at a valuation of $13 billion or more, according to reporting over the weekend; no bidder has been named publicly, and the company is working with banks to evaluate offers. That's nearly triple the $4.5 billion valuation from its 2023 round led by Salesforce Ventures, and comes after Hugging Face turned down a $500 million investment from Nvidia earlier this year that would have valued it at $7 billion. CEO Clem Delangue said the company is "close to profitability" and has "only recently started to touch the money" raised three years ago.

*Why it matters:* Hugging Face is the default distribution hub for open-source AI models — a sale would hand a strategic chokepoint in the AI stack to whoever wins the bidding, following Stripe's $7 billion purchase of OpenRouter earlier this year.

## 2. Nvidia is negotiating a $30 billion stake in Perplexity
**Nvidia / Perplexity · Aug 23, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to invest in Perplexity at a valuation topping $30 billion, up from about $121 million in April 2023 — roughly 175x growth in 30 months. Perplexity's annualized revenue passed $750 million this month, up from under $250 million seven months ago, driven largely by Perplexity Computer, a browser- and desktop-automation agent it launched in February. Nvidia reportedly considered simply licensing Perplexity's technology before shifting toward an equity stake, a pattern it has also used with Poolside and Groq.

*Why it matters:* Nvidia is moving beyond selling the compute AI runs on and into owning pieces of the software built on top of it.

## 3. XPeng's robotics unit raised $900 million at a $6.3 billion valuation
**XPeng · Aug 24, 2026 · 1 day ago**

XPeng's humanoid-robotics business closed its first outside funding round, raising more than $900 million — about $600 million from external investors including IDG Capital, Gaorong Ventures, Tencent, and Alibaba, with the rest from an XPeng subsidiary and company leadership. XPeng called it the largest private-equity round yet in China's "embodied intelligence" sector. The money funds R&D on XPeng's Iron humanoid robot, due to enter mass production by the end of 2026 ahead of commercial deployment in 2027.

*Why it matters:* It's a concrete sign that humanoid-robot investment is shifting from lab demos to real production financing, and that Chinese automakers are treating robotics as a second core business.

## 4. LandSpace became the third company ever to land an orbital rocket booster
**LandSpace · Aug 19, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

LandSpace landed the first-stage booster of its Zhuque-3 rocket in Gansu Province roughly eight minutes after liftoff on August 19, making it the third company in the world — after SpaceX and Blue Origin — to recover an orbital-class booster, and the first Chinese company to do it. The startup pulled it off on only its second orbital attempt, eight months after its first booster exploded during a landing try.

*Why it matters:* Reusable boosters are what let SpaceX undercut every competitor on launch cost; LandSpace closing that gap changes the economics of China's commercial launch industry.

## 5. Amazon is expanding drone delivery to nearly 500 US cities
**Amazon · Aug 19, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Amazon said Prime Air will expand from 11 metro areas across seven states to nearly 500 US cities and towns by the end of 2026 — a sixfold jump — with Chicago, Atlanta, Cleveland, Syracuse, and Boise launching soon. The drones carry packages up to 5 pounds, covering more than 60% of Amazon's most commonly ordered items, with delivery in as fast as 30 minutes and free shipping on Prime orders over $50.

*Why it matters:* This is the clearest sign yet that drone delivery has moved past pilot-program status into a shipping option built for tens of millions of households.
