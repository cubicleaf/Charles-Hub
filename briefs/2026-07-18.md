*Today's theme: the frontier race is shifting from who ships the best model to who controls the compute, capital, and coding benchmarks behind it.*

## 1. Google delays Gemini 3.5 Pro, Alphabet drops on the news
**Google · July 16 · 2 days ago**

Gemini 3.5 Pro, announced at I/O in May for a June rollout, is now delayed several months after internal testing found its coding performance fell short of Google's targets. Alphabet shares fell as much as 4%, plus 1.5% pre-market, after CNBC's report. A spokesperson said Google remains "focused on delivering advanced AI models while keeping them cost-effective." The delay lands as Meta pushes its Muse Spark 1.1 coding model and OpenAI touts GPT-5.6 Sol as far more token-efficient on agentic coding work.

*Why it matters:* Coding ability, not chat quality, is now the metric labs miss deadlines over — and the one investors price in real time.

## 2. Meta hires away AWS's cloud and AI infrastructure chief
**Meta · July 17 · 1 day ago**

Dave Brown, the AWS senior vice president who ran EC2 and Amazon's compute and ML services for nearly 19 years, is leaving to join Meta's infrastructure team within weeks to lead "Meta Compute," a prospective service renting Meta's AI data-center capacity to outside customers. Zuckerberg has called a Meta cloud business "definitely on the table," backed by $125-145 billion in planned 2026 capex.

*Why it matters:* Meta hiring the executive who built AWS's compute business signals it wants to become a cloud seller, not just a hyperscale buyer.

## 3. Apple's trade-secrets suit against OpenAI widens
**Apple · July 10 · Still developing**

Apple's federal lawsuit alleges OpenAI's hardware chief, ex-Apple VP Tang Tan, directed job candidates to bring "actual parts" to interviews and coached departing staff on evading exit security; Apple says over 400 former employees now work at OpenAI. OpenAI says it's "not aware of any evidence" the claims have merit. This week's coverage ties the case to OpenAI's expected IPO.

*Why it matters:* A hardware-poaching dispute between two of Apple's biggest AI partners is now a legal overhang heading into OpenAI's most-watched fundraising event.

## 4. UK robotics startup Humanoid jumps to a $1.2B valuation
**Humanoid · July 16 · 2 days ago**

London-based Humanoid closed the first tranche of a $150 million Series A valuing the robot maker at $1.2 billion, up from a roughly $1 billion ask days earlier, and is reportedly in talks with Saudi Aramco for further backing at unicorn terms.

*Why it matters:* Humanoid-robotics valuations are now moving on the same weekly cadence as AI-model funding rounds, not the slower hardware-startup timeline investors are used to.

## 5. General Fusion becomes the first publicly traded fusion company
**General Fusion · July 17 · Still developing**

General Fusion began trading on Nasdaq July 13 and rang the opening bell July 17, entering public markets with roughly $150 million in cash earmarked for its Lawson-machine program through 2028 milestones. The listing follows a Bloomberg tally showing global fusion investment climbed 69% to a record $4.5 billion over the past year.

*Why it matters:* Fusion is starting to raise money the way AI labs do — in public markets — well before any company has delivered grid power.
