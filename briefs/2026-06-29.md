*The week opens with AI talent abandoning Google en masse, OpenAI retiring another model, and SpaceX spending its IPO money before the confetti settled.*

## 1. Google DeepMind Loses Four Senior Researchers in Six Days
**Google DeepMind · June 18–24 · 5–11 days ago · Still developing**

Noam Shazeer departed for OpenAI; John Jumper (2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, AlphaFold lead), Jonas Adler, and Alexander Pritzel all moved to Anthropic — four departures in six days with no announced replacements. Alphabet shed roughly $269 billion in market capitalization across the week. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis has not commented publicly.

*Why it matters:* Three Nobel-prize-adjacent researchers landing at a single competitor in one week is unprecedented; it reshapes Anthropic's research depth and signals a retention crisis Google has so far declined to address.

## 2. GPT-4.5 Retired; GPT-5.5 Is Now ChatGPT's Default for All Users
**OpenAI · June 27 · 2 days ago**

OpenAI completed a planned model sunset on June 27, migrating all GPT-4.5 conversations to GPT-5.5. Free and paid tiers now run the same baseline model. The retirement removes one of the cleaner external comparison points for tracking regression between OpenAI generations.

*Why it matters:* With only GPT-5.5 available, independent benchmarking against a stable OpenAI reference becomes harder — OpenAI increasingly controls the frame for its own performance narrative.

## 3. SpaceX's Record $86B IPO, Then $60B Cursor Buy Two Days Later
**SpaceX · June 12–16 · 13 days ago · Still developing**

SpaceX priced at $135/share, raised $86.2 billion (largest IPO on record), then used appreciating SPCX stock to acquire AI coding startup Cursor in an all-stock deal worth $60B at signing — roughly $77B at current prices. Deal expected to close Q3 2026. Cursor folds into SpaceX's AI division built around xAI, which SpaceX merged with earlier this year.

*Why it matters:* A rocket company immediately redeploying IPO capital to buy the leading AI code editor signals that SpaceX/xAI is building a full-stack developer platform, not just a model.

## 4. Apple Rebuilds Siri on Gemini; Claude Gets a Native iPhone Spot
**Apple · June 8 (WWDC) · 21 days ago · Still developing**

Tim Cook's final WWDC keynote unveiled a Siri whose cloud queries route through a custom Gemini model (~1.2T parameters, ~$1B/year licensing deal). iOS 27 adds "Extensions" letting users set third-party AI assistants — including Claude — as defaults for specific tasks; the first time Anthropic's model reaches iPhone natively. Extensions are in developer beta, shipping fall 2026.

*Why it matters:* Apple's install base puts the Extensions winner in front of ~1.5 billion active devices; a default slot there dwarfs anything achievable through API volume alone.

## 5. Z.ai GLM-5.2: MIT-Licensed Open Weights Beat GPT-5.5 on Coding
**Z.ai · June 13 · 16 days ago**

Z.ai released GLM-5.2, a 753B-parameter MoE model, as open weights under MIT license on Hugging Face. SWE-bench Pro: 62.1 vs. GPT-5.5's 58.6; FrontierSWE: 74.4% vs. Claude Opus 4.8's 75.1%. Its IndexShare architecture cuts per-token FLOPs 2.9× at 1M-token context. Enterprise API access starts at $12.60/month.

*Why it matters:* A freely downloadable model that outperforms GPT-5.5 on the coding benchmark most enterprises prioritize breaks the assumption that frontier coding performance requires frontier pricing.

## Under the radar

**Microsoft Majorana 2 quantum chip draws expert skepticism · June 2–3 · 27 days ago**
Microsoft claims Majorana 2 achieves topological qubit lifetimes exceeding 20 seconds (up from milliseconds) after replacing aluminum with lead in the material stack. Independent physicists remain unpersuaded — the preprint is unreviewed and Microsoft retracted a prior topological claim in 2021. Roadmap target: scalable quantum by 2029.
*Why it matters:* If claims survive peer review, it clears the primary barrier to fault-tolerant quantum computing; if they don't, it extends a multi-year pattern of premature topological announcements.

**Colorado AI Act delayed to January 1, 2027**
Governor Polis signed SB 26-189 in May, scaling back and delaying the original Colorado AI Act (the first U.S. state AI liability law) after a federal court stay and White House pressure to diverge from the EU framework. Revised law adds a 60-day cure period and a 2030 sunset clause.
*Why it matters:* Each delay shifts leverage toward federal preemption and reduces the urgency for AI developers to build compliance infrastructure now.
