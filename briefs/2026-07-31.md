*Today's theme is the physical cost of the AI buildout: an earthquake exposes how concentrated chip manufacturing still is, while capital races to build, connect, and control the infrastructure under it.*

## 1. 7.1 earthquake in Japan halts TSMC, Sony, Tokyo Electron chip plants
**Kumamoto, Japan · July 28-29, 2026 · 2-3 days ago**

A magnitude-7.1 earthquake struck Kumamoto prefecture, Japan's second-largest chip cluster, killing at least 34 people. TSMC's JASM fab there — about 3% of its total capacity — took intensity-5 shaking but no structural damage and is gradually resuming output, with expansion construction paused. Tokyo Electron shut both its Kumamoto facilities for inspection, Sony and Renesas are still assessing their plants, and Honda and Toyota halted nearby auto lines. Analysts see limited direct fab damage but flag transport and power disruption as the real risk.

*Why it matters:* Kumamoto exists to diversify chipmaking away from a single point of failure in Taiwan — this is a live test of whether that bet holds up.

## 2. Meta's free cash flow craters as 2026 AI capex rises to $145B
**Meta · July 29, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Meta narrowed its 2026 capex range upward to $130B-$145B and posted Q2 capital spending of $31.08 billion, up 83% year-over-year. Free cash flow fell to $784 million from $8.55 billion a year earlier despite operating cash flow rising 25%. The stock dropped on the guidance.

*Why it matters:* Meta is the sharpest evidence yet that AI infrastructure spending is now outrunning the cash the business generates.

## 3. Nvidia-backed Nscale buys AI software startup Anyscale for $1.65B
**Nscale · July 30, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Nscale agreed to acquire Anyscale, built around the open-source Ray framework for distributing AI workloads across clusters, in a deal worth about $1.65 billion. Anyscale's roughly 200 employees join Nscale once the deal closes in the second half of 2026, giving Nscale its own software layer ahead of a possible IPO.

*Why it matters:* AI cloud providers are racing to own the software stack, not just rent out GPUs, before they go public.

## 4. EU opens tender for seven AI gigafactories to close US, China gap
**European Commission · July 30, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Brussels opened bidding for up to seven AI "gigafactories," a €30 billion push with €10 billion from EU and national governments and the rest from private investors. Four smaller sites need 75,000 chips each for up to €500 million; three larger sites need 100,000 chips for up to €1 billion. Bidding closes November 12; awards land in early 2027.

*Why it matters:* Brussels is putting real public money behind AI sovereignty rather than just talking about it, though the payoff is still well over a year out.

## 5. GlobalFoundries wins $300M to speed up connections between AI chips
**GlobalFoundries / US Dept. of Commerce · July 29, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Washington will award GlobalFoundries $300 million in CHIPS Act funding to scale up silicon photonics — using light instead of electrical signals between chips — targeting 400 gigabits per second and up to 5x better energy efficiency than current designs.

*Why it matters:* Compute keeps getting faster, but moving data between chips is becoming the real bottleneck — Washington is funding the plumbing, not another GPU.
