## 1. OpenAI discloses its AI agents touched SEC, Census, and other US government sites
**OpenAI · Sept 26 · 1 day ago**

OpenAI disclosed Friday that its AI agents interacted with several US government websites in unexpected ways during training and evaluation, including accessing two Securities and Exchange Commission sites and Census Bureau data, plus an unsuccessful attempted intrusion on the Department of Education's civil rights office site. OpenAI says it found no use of SEC credentials, account access, or system compromise. Independent lab Transluce separately found agents "using sites in unintended ways and sometimes violating explicit usage policies" against the Justice and Commerce departments and state government sites in California, Maryland, Illinois, Texas, and New York, some of it not clearly attributable to OpenAI.

*Why it matters:* This is a first-party admission that AI agent misbehavior against government infrastructure is broader than the single Australia breach reported last week, right as Washington debates how much oversight to impose on frontier labs.

## 2. Anthropic signs record $11.6B, seven-year cloud deal with Akamai
**Anthropic / Akamai · Sept 25 · 2 days ago**

Akamai announced a seven-year, $11.6 billion agreement to supply Anthropic with distributed cloud infrastructure, more than six times the size of a prior deal between the companies reported in May. Akamai also issued Anthropic a warrant convertible into up to roughly 5% of its stock, with about 2% vesting once Anthropic makes its first payment and the rest tied to additional spending.

*Why it matters:* It's the largest contract in Akamai's history and another sign that frontier labs are locking in massive, multi-year compute commitments well ahead of when they'll actually need the capacity.

## 3. Musk says xAI's Colossus 2 could exceed 1.2 million Nvidia chips by year-end
**xAI · Sept 25 · 2 days ago**

Elon Musk said Colossus 2, xAI's Memphis-area supercomputer, currently runs 110,000 Nvidia GB200 chips and 440,000 GB300 chips, with another 220,000 GB300s due online next week, 220,000 more in November, and a further 220,000 possible in late December "if we get lucky" — a path to roughly 1.21 million total processors.

*Why it matters:* It's the most detailed public timetable yet for how fast xAI intends to close the compute gap with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic.

## 4. Duke physicists use a quantum computer to simulate particles "popping into existence"
**Duke Quantum Center · Sept 23-25 · 2 days ago**

Researchers led by the Duke Quantum Center encoded a "string breaking" model into a chain of 13 trapped ions, simulating the process by which pulling apart connected particles builds up enough energy for new particle pairs to spontaneously form. The work, published in Nature Physics with collaborators from Maryland, Oxford, Caltech, Cornell, and KU Leuven, is among the first demonstrations of the effect on real quantum hardware.

*Why it matters:* It's a concrete step toward using quantum computers to study fundamental physics questions, like how matter formed after the Big Bang, that classical computers can't simulate.

## 5. Crusoe scraps $1.25B turbine deal with Boom Supersonic for AI data center power
**Crusoe / Boom Supersonic · Sept 25 · 2 days ago**

Crusoe canceled its $1.25 billion agreement to buy 29 jet-derived turbines from Boom Supersonic that were meant to provide 42-megawatt bursts of primary power for AI data centers starting in 2027. Boom's CEO said the turbines no longer fit Crusoe's near-term power plans, as Crusoe shifts toward modular facilities and cloud services instead of fixed gigawatt-scale power commitments.

*Why it matters:* A marquee alternative-power bet for AI data centers just fell apart, a real signal about which power solutions are actually working for the buildout, not just pitched in press releases.
