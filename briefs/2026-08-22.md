## 1. Anthropic targets an IPO that could beat SpaceX's record raise
**Anthropic · Aug 20, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Anthropic has told bankers it expects its IPO to match or exceed SpaceX's record raise of $75 billion, which grew to roughly $86.2 billion once overallotment options were exercised, and could file publicly as soon as the end of August. Its annualized revenue run rate hit $65 billion in July, up from $787 million a year earlier, though the company posted a net loss of roughly $42 billion in 2025. Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan Chase are underwriting the offering, and Anthropic is separately pursuing a revolving credit facility above its $10 billion target.

*Why it matters:* A raise anywhere near that range would be the largest tech IPO ever and would set the price benchmark the rest of the AI-lab sector, including OpenAI's eventual listing, gets measured against.

## 2. Nvidia strikes $7B deal to license and hire from Poolside
**Nvidia / Poolside · Aug 21, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Nvidia is paying $6 billion for a non-exclusive license to Poolside's "Model Factory" coding-model platform and investing another $1 billion in the startup, valuing it at $12 billion, up from $3 billion previously. About 109 Poolside employees are moving to Nvidia while the startup's three co-founders keep running it independently. It's the third deal of this shape from Nvidia, after a reported $20 billion arrangement with Groq and a smaller one with Enfabrica.

*Why it matters:* Structuring the deal as licensing plus hiring, instead of an outright acquisition, lets Nvidia absorb technology and talent while sidestepping the antitrust review a straight buyout would trigger.

## 3. Nevada approves 8,000 robotaxis for Tesla, Waymo, and Uber
**Nevada Transportation Authority · Aug 20, 2026 · 2 days ago**

The Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved permits letting Tesla, Waymo, and Uber (via Motional and Zoox) run up to 8,000 robotaxis combined in Clark County over the next 12 months — 5,000 for Tesla, 1,000 each for Waymo and Uber. Tesla's own chief engineer said the 5,000 figure "has always been a ceiling" for the company, and expects a real deployment closer to half that. Local taxi operators opposed the permits over crowding concerns.

*Why it matters:* It's the largest single robotaxi authorization in the U.S. to date, and the real test now is whether any of the three operators can actually field fleets that large.

## 4. China's humanoid robot makers now ship 97% of the world's units
**Industry reports · Aug 20, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Multiple industry trackers put China's share of global humanoid robot shipments at around 97% for the first half of 2026, with total global shipments roughly tripling year over year. Agibot alone shipped more than 8,000 units to take roughly 44% of the global market, overtaking Hangzhou-based Unitree as the top individual shipper.

*Why it matters:* The humanoid-robot race is turning into a manufacturing and supply-chain contest as much as an AI one, and China's lead here looks wider than its lead in frontier models.

## 5. Mystery model Ox Alpha tops coding benchmarks, free for a week
**OpenRouter / OpenCode · Aug 20, 2026 · 2 days ago**

An anonymous model called Ox Alpha appeared on OpenRouter and OpenCode with a 1,048,576-token context window and free access for about a week — the fifth unlabeled "stealth" model release in six months. One developer's informal 10-task test put it ahead of GPT-5.6 and Claude Fable on a coding benchmark, though that's a small, unaudited sample rather than a leaderboard result. Tokenizer and video-module fingerprints point to a Chinese lab, possibly Zhipu's next GLM.

*Why it matters:* Stealth-testing frontier models on public routers before naming them is now a routine go-to-market tactic, which makes early benchmark claims about new models harder to take at face value.
