*AI's compute build-out turned concrete this week — a chip mega-factory, a 10-trillion-parameter run, and a hardware buyout — while Meta absorbed its largest child-safety penalty yet.*

## 1. Tesla and SpaceX commit $16.8B to a Texas chip mega-factory
**Tesla / SpaceX · Aug 6 · 2 days ago**

Tesla and SpaceX committed $16.8 billion to build "Terafab," a 100-million-square-foot chip factory in Grimes County, Texas — Musk called it "the largest and most valuable building on Earth." It will make chips for Optimus robots, Cybercabs, and SpaceX's space-based data centers, employing at least 3,000 people; total investment could reach $119 billion across later phases, with Intel contributing fab expertise.

*Why it matters:* One of the largest single chip-manufacturing commitments on record — a bet that AI-chip demand from robots, cars, and orbital data centers justifies fab capacity outside the traditional foundry model.

## 2. ByteDance pretrains a 10-trillion-parameter model to chase Anthropic
**ByteDance · Aug 7 · 1 day ago**

The Financial Times reported ByteDance is pretraining an AI model with as many as 10 trillion parameters — over three times Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — meant to rival Anthropic's frontier Mythos system. Pretraining runs three to six months, with final size undecided; founder Zhang Yiming told the roughly 2,000-person Seed team to build genuine frontier capability, not distill existing models.

*Why it matters:* China's clearest signal yet that raw scale, not just efficiency shortcuts, remains the plan for closing the gap with US labs, despite export controls limiting the chips available to train at this size.

## 3. AMD buys Taalas to hardwire AI models into silicon
**AMD · Aug 6 · 2 days ago**

AMD acquired Toronto-based Taalas, which etches specific AI models directly into silicon instead of general-purpose GPUs; terms weren't disclosed. Taalas, founded in 2023 by a former AMD and Nvidia chip architect and built on TSMC's 6nm process, says its chips beat Nvidia's H200 and B200 on tokens per user at a tenth of the power; it raised $169 million in February. AMD will fold the tech into its Instinct GPU roadmap.

*Why it matters:* AMD's answer to Nvidia's $20 billion Groq acquisition earlier this year — both bet that model-specific silicon, not general-purpose GPUs, wins the next phase of the AI hardware race.

## 4. Meta's New Mexico child-safety bill hits $942M total
**Meta · Aug 6–7 · 1–2 days ago**

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to fund $567 million in youth mental-health treatment and screening — $420 million for direct treatment — on top of $375 million in civil penalties a jury imposed in March, bringing total liability to $942 million. The judge also ordered AI-based age-estimation tools, an under-13 prediction model within two years, and twice-yearly compliance reporting.

*Why it matters:* First case to pair a nine-figure penalty with court-mandated product changes — a template other state attorneys general are likely to copy.

## 5. SpaceX rocket stage confirmed to have hit the Moon
**SpaceX · Aug 5 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

A discarded SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage, adrift for roughly seven months, struck the Moon's far side near Einstein Crater at about 5,400 mph. Telescopes confirmed the debris plume, and South Korea's Danuri orbiter captured follow-up images; SpaceX said solar activity and lunar gravity put the 45-foot stage on its collision course.

*Why it matters:* One of only a handful of confirmed artificial Moon impacts, and a reminder that a growing launch cadence leaves more hardware adrift.

## Under the radar
Firmus, an Australian AI-infrastructure builder, raised $2 billion led by Nvidia, Coatue, and Blackstone, pushing its valuation above $10.5 billion — nearly double April's figure — to expand AI data centers across Asia-Pacific.

*Why it matters:* Investors are backing the physical build-out of AI infrastructure in new regions, not just the model labs themselves.
