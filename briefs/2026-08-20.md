## 1. LandSpace lands China's first reusable orbital-class rocket booster
**LandSpace / Gansu Province, China · Aug 19, 2026 · 1 day ago**

LandSpace's Zhuque-3 rocket launched from the Dongfeng Commercial Space Innovation Pilot Zone and landed its 76.6-meter first stage vertically at a site in Minqin County — the first recovery of an orbital-class booster on landing legs by any Chinese company. The methane-fueled, stainless-steel rocket is taller than SpaceX's Falcon 9; LandSpace is targeting up to 20 reuses per booster. A fire was visible in the booster's aft section after touchdown, but the landing itself succeeded on the vehicle's second flight.

*Why it matters:* SpaceX has flown reusable boosters for a decade with little real competition; LandSpace joining that short list — as a private company, not a state program — is the clearest sign yet that China's commercial space sector is closing the gap.

## 2. Unitree's IPO pops 629%, valuing the robot maker near $66 billion
**Unitree Robotics / Shanghai STAR Market · Aug 19, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Unitree priced its IPO at 150.80 yuan a share, raising about 6.1 billion yuan ($905 million), then opened 629% above that price on its Shanghai debut — briefly valuing the company at roughly $66 billion before shares settled to close up 460% on the day. The retail tranche was oversubscribed more than 8,000 times, a record for a STAR Market tech listing. Unitree is the first humanoid-robot maker to list on a mainland Chinese exchange, backed by Tencent, Alibaba, Meituan and DeepSeek.

*Why it matters:* It's the strongest public-markets verdict yet on humanoid robotics as an investable category, and it puts China's leading robot maker on a mainland exchange years ahead of any US rival going public.

## 3. CISA gives federal agencies a same-day deadline on a critical Ray flaw
**CISA · Aug 18, 2026 · 2 days ago**

CISA added CVE-2025-62593, a 9.4-severity remote-code-execution flaw in the open-source Ray machine-learning framework, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Aug. 18 and gave federal civilian agencies until today, Aug. 20, to patch it. The bug lets an attacker bypass Ray's browser check via DNS rebinding and run code on unpatched clusters; CISA confirmed active exploitation. Ray scales Python and ML workloads and is used by Amazon, Apple and OpenAI, among others.

*Why it matters:* A three-day patch window on infrastructure this widely used — Ray reports millions of weekly downloads — is a sign of how seriously CISA is treating AI-training infrastructure as critical infrastructure now.

## 4. Nvidia's H200 chips start reaching China again in small shipments
**Nvidia · Aug 19-20, 2026 · Still developing**

Nvidia's H200 AI chips have begun moving into China in limited quantities, with ByteDance and Tencent each reportedly receiving around 10,000 units so far, per reporting cited by the Financial Times. US officials have cleared purchases of up to 100,000 chips per company, but Beijing has been steering firms to route hardware through Hong Kong rather than the mainland, to protect domestic chipmakers. Nvidia is said to be holding roughly 500,000 H200 units earmarked for Chinese buyers ahead of its Aug. 26 earnings report.

*Why it matters:* Even small, closely watched shipments signal a real loosening in the chip-export standoff, with a market some analysts value at up to $30 billion in potential sales riding on how far it goes.

## 5. OpenAI pauses its biggest frontier training run over cyber risk
**OpenAI · Aug 18-19, 2026 · Still developing**

OpenAI said its largest planned frontier reinforcement-learning run remains on hold while it runs smaller-scale training and evaluations to validate safeguards, after determining on Aug. 7 that its unreleased Astra model could reach a "critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. The company is expanding monitoring of Astra's tool use and targeting security-team alerts within 30 minutes of a flagged concern, following tightened safeguards put in place after a security incident involving Hugging Face.

*Why it matters:* This is a frontier lab choosing to slow down its own most capable unreleased model because of what it can do, not because a regulator forced it to — a real test of whether self-imposed safety pacing holds up under competitive pressure.
