*AI safety thresholds and AI infrastructure spending both set new marks this week, while a familiar enterprise VPN line keeps getting broken into.*

## 1. OpenAI Classifies Astra as Its First "Critical"-Risk Cybersecurity Model
**OpenAI · Sept 2 · 1 day ago**

OpenAI disclosed that its upcoming Astra model is the first to trigger the top "critical" tier of its Preparedness Framework for cyber capability: in testing it scored 100% on ExploitBench, independently found and used two zero-day vulnerabilities from a set of 20 high-severity flaws, and chained weaknesses to escalate from a standard user to root on hardened systems. OpenAI paused development to add chain-of-thought monitoring for risky actions, which lifted Astra's refusal rate in cyber-jailbreak evaluations to 91.5%, up from 59% for its predecessor.

*Why it matters:* it's the first time a frontier lab has said one of its own models crossed the highest capability threshold it defined for real-world cyberattacks, not a hypothetical one.

## 2. Dell's AI Server Backlog Hits $95 Billion After a Record Quarter
**Dell · Sept 1 · 2 days ago**

Dell's fiscal Q2 revenue rose 58% year-over-year to $46.97 billion, beating estimates, with AI-optimized server revenue of $16.4 billion — roughly double the prior year. The company booked $60.9 billion in new AI server orders during the quarter alone, pushing its total backlog to $95 billion, and raised full-year revenue guidance by $25 billion to around $192 billion.

*Why it matters:* this is demand measured in signed orders and shipped hardware, a harder data point than the sentiment debate over whether AI infrastructure spending is overbuilt.

## 3. Google Ships Gemini 3.8 Flash, Its Third Budget Model in Six Weeks
**Google DeepMind · Sept 2 · 1 day ago**

Gemini 3.8 Flash scores 59 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tying GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.6, and hits 73.7% on the DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon coding benchmark — just behind Claude Opus 5's 74.0% and ahead of its own predecessor's 65.3%. It's priced at $0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens through year-end.

*Why it matters:* Google is out-cadencing rivals on mid-tier models, compressing margins across the budget-API segment other labs compete in.

## 4. Uber Cuts 3,300 Jobs to Fund Its Robotaxi Push
**Uber · Sept 2 · 1 day ago**

Uber is laying off about 3,300 people, roughly 10% of staff and its largest cut since the pandemic, while shrinking management layers by 20% and eliminating most one- or two-person teams. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi tied the overhaul to a pledge of more than $10 billion toward robotaxi partnerships in the coming years.

*Why it matters:* a major platform company is restructuring its core workforce specifically around autonomous-vehicle competition, not general cost-cutting.

## 5. SonicWall's SMA1000 Appliances Hit by a Third Zero-Day Chain in a Year
**SonicWall · Sept 1 · 2 days ago**

SonicWall warned that a pre-authentication SSRF flaw (CVSS 10.0) in its SMA1000 Work Place interface is being chained with a post-auth remote-code-execution bug to achieve unauthenticated RCE on SMA1000 6210, 7210, and 8200v appliances. Hotfixes are available; this is the third distinct SMA1000 zero-day attack chain disclosed in under a year, after incidents in July and December 2025.

*Why it matters:* enterprise remote-access appliances remain one of the most reliable initial-access footholds for attackers, and a third strike against the same product line is a real trust problem for SonicWall's customers.

## Under the radar
**Aesto Health disclosed a breach affecting more than 9.5 million patients**, stolen from its AWS infrastructure in December 2025 and confirmed internally in May. Exposed data includes Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, and financial and health records across at least two dozen healthcare-provider clients. *Why it matters:* SaaS vendors that aggregate patient data across many providers are becoming single points of catastrophic failure.

**IonQ, NVIDIA, and qBraid demonstrated a 54% reduction in logical error rates** for a quantum chemistry simulation, combining IonQ's trapped-ion hardware with NVIDIA GPU-accelerated error mitigation rather than physical error correction alone. *Why it matters:* hybrid quantum-classical error mitigation, not full fault tolerance, is the near-term path to useful quantum chemistry runs.
