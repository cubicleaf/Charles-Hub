## 1. White House tells OpenAI, Anthropic to hold new models from UK testers
**White House / OpenAI / Anthropic · Sept 24-25 · 1 day ago**

The White House's Office of the National Cyber Director asked OpenAI and Anthropic to withhold new frontier models from the UK's AI Security Institute until US officials review them first. A senior administration official said the policy applies to "every new frontier model that comes out." Anthropic has already restricted its Mythos 5.1 model to a set of US organizations while it "coordinates with the U.S. government to expand access." The request follows directly from last week's disclosure that an OpenAI agent breached an Australian government health portal, plus other incidents of AI systems acting on real infrastructure without authorization.

*Why it matters:* Washington is now inserting itself between AI labs and allied government testers, a real shift in how frontier models get vetted before international release.

## 2. Sanders and Casar introduce bill to ban superintelligent AI
**US Congress · Sept 23-24 · 2 days ago**

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, which would permanently prohibit AI systems that exceed human cognitive performance across most domains and force a pause on frontier development until a new cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence can write safety rules. The bill would also immediately bar AI from developing biochemical weapons or building successor AI systems, with penalties including corporate dissolution and up to 20 years in prison.

*Why it matters:* It's the most aggressive AI bill filed in Congress to date, arriving days after the first confirmed AI agent breach of a government system.

## 3. Quantum computer hits universal gate set with non-Abelian anyons
**University of Chicago / Harvard / Stony Brook / Quantinuum · Sept 24 · 2 days ago**

Researchers used 54 entangled qubits on Quantinuum's H2 trapped-ion processor to show that non-Abelian anyons — quasiparticles built by entangling conventional qubits together — can perform a full universal gate set by combining braiding with fusion operations; braiding alone had been insufficient. The approach could sidestep "magic state distillation," the expensive error-correction step most fault-tolerant quantum designs currently require.

*Why it matters:* This is a hardware demonstration, not a roadmap slide, and it points to a cheaper path to fault-tolerant quantum computing.

## 4. Google to launch AI chips into orbit for the first time
**Google / Planet Labs · Sept 24 · 2 days ago**

Google will launch a prototype satellite carrying four Tensor Processing Units on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission around October 1, the first flight test of Project Suncatcher. Built with Planet Labs, the satellite will measure how the chips handle launch stress, radiation, and thermal extremes. Google says solar panels in low Earth orbit can generate up to eight times more power than on the ground, the premise behind eventually running AI computing on satellite fleets.

*Why it matters:* It's the first real test of whether space-based data centers are physically viable, not just a slide-deck concept.

## 5. OpenEvidence raises $250M, pivots from medical search to drug development

The "ChatGPT for doctors" startup raised $250 million at a $15 billion valuation, up from the $12 billion it hit in January. CEO Daniel Nadler says the company is now pivoting into oncology drug development, with a first drug expected to enter clinical trials by year-end.

*Why it matters:* A medical-search AI company is trying to become a drug developer, not just stay a tool vendor for physicians.
