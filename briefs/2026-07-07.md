*Today's theme: governments and AI labs are renegotiating who holds the leash, on stage in Geneva and in backroom equity talks in Washington.*

## 1. UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance opens in Geneva
**UN · July 6-7 · 1 day ago**

The UN's first Global Dialogue on AI Governance opened in Geneva, bringing delegates from all 193 member states together with industry and civil society on shared rules for frontier AI safety, human rights, and accountability. No binding agreements are expected from this first session; a second round follows in New York in May 2027.

*Why it matters:* This is where AI governance stops being a national talking point and starts getting negotiated as an international framework.

## 2. OpenAI proposes handing the US government a 5% stake
**OpenAI · July 2 · 5 days ago · Still developing**

The Financial Times reported OpenAI pitched giving the US government a 5% equity stake, worth roughly $42.6 billion at its $852 billion valuation, with Sam Altman framing it as sharing AI's upside with the public. The plan envisions Anthropic, Google, and Meta ceding similar stakes through a sovereign-wealth vehicle; talks are preliminary, and Anthropic says it hasn't discussed the idea with the administration.

*Why it matters:* A government equity stake in frontier labs would blur the line between regulator and stakeholder.

## 3. Gemini 3.5 Pro rolls out with a 2-million-token context window
**Google · July 6 · 1 day ago · Still developing**

Gemini 3.5 Pro, unveiled at I/O in May, is finally reaching general availability after missing its June target twice. It carries a 2-million-token context window — double Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8 — plus a Deep Think reasoning mode; Google hasn't published benchmarks yet.

*Why it matters:* Context-window size is becoming the axis frontier labs compete on as benchmark scores converge.

## 4. Tesla launches unsupervised Robotaxi service in Miami
**Tesla · July 3 · 4 days ago · Still developing**

Tesla began fully driverless Robotaxi rides across roughly 20 square miles of western Miami-Dade County, the first city where it skipped the human-monitor phase from day one. The Model Y fleet now faces Florida's sudden downpours and glare — the exact conditions NHTSA cited in March when it escalated a camera-only FSD probe to an engineering analysis, the step before a possible recall.

*Why it matters:* Miami tests whether Tesla's camera-only approach generalizes to weather Texas and Arizona never throw at it.

## 5. Realta Fusion converts plasma directly into electricity
**Realta Fusion · June 19 demo, reported June 30 · Still developing**

Realta Fusion says it became the first private fusion company to convert plasma kinetic energy directly into electricity, harvesting several amps at 100 volts from its WHAM reactor in Wisconsin — enough to power a few lightbulbs, skipping the steam-turbine step every other reactor design relies on.

*Why it matters:* Direct conversion, if it scales, removes one of the most expensive, lossy components from a fusion power plant.

## Under the radar
**Meta · July 1 · 6 days ago.** Bloomberg reported Meta is building a cloud business to resell its excess AI compute, competing with AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud; Meta shares jumped over 10% while CoreWeave and Nebius each fell more than 10%. *Why it matters:* An admission that Meta's AI infrastructure spend has outpaced its own product needs.

**Agility Robotics · June 24 deal, July 5 follow-up.** Agility Robotics is going public via a $2.5 billion SPAC merger, the largest capital raise in humanoid-robotics history, though its CEO is downplaying near-term home-robot expectations. *Why it matters:* The first pure-play humanoid robotics company on public markets.
