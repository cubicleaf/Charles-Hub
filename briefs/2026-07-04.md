*Washington is inserting itself into how frontier AI gets built, released, and owned — three stories today point the same direction.*

## 1. OpenAI proposes the US government take a 5% stake
**OpenAI · July 2 · 2 days ago**

OpenAI has pitched giving the US government a 5% equity stake, worth roughly $42.6 billion at its $852 billion valuation, per the Financial Times. Altman reportedly wants Anthropic, Google, and Meta to make similar contributions to an Alaska Permanent Fund-style vehicle paying public dividends from AI profits. The idea is conceptual, unagreed to by other labs, and would likely need an act of Congress.

*Why it matters:* Even a partial version rewires the relationship between frontier labs and the state, turning government from regulator into shareholder.

## 2. Google's €4.1B Android fine becomes permanent
**EU Court of Justice · July 2 · 2 days ago**

Europe's highest court dismissed Google's final appeal, locking in a €4.125 billion fine for bundling Search and Chrome into Android's Play Store licensing terms. The ruling is binding with no further appeal available, and it activates the EU's Antitrust Damages Directive, opening the door to follow-on civil suits from rivals across 13 EEA countries.

*Why it matters:* This isn't just a fine anymore — it's a green light for a new wave of private antitrust litigation against Google across Europe.

## 3. Anthropic explores building its own AI chip with Samsung
**Anthropic / Samsung · July 2 · 2 days ago**

Anthropic is in early talks with Samsung to manufacture a custom AI inference accelerator on Samsung's 2nm process, per Bloomberg and The Information. No specs, prototypes, or timeline exist yet, but Anthropic recently hired a second chip engineer from OpenAI's hardware team. Samsung was also a strategic investor in Anthropic's last funding round.

*Why it matters:* Every major lab — OpenAI with Broadcom's "Jalapeño," now Anthropic — is moving to cut Nvidia dependence, a real shift in AI's supply chain.

## 4. GPT-5.6's limited rollout tests new US frontier-model rules
**OpenAI / White House · June 26 · 8 days ago · Still developing**

OpenAI is holding back GPT-5.6's full release after the government invoked a June 2 executive order allowing it to request up to 30 days of early access to "covered frontier models." Altman says he doesn't object to safety testing but doesn't want the government picking his customers. The White House is now finalizing broader voluntary standards for frontier releases, with an announcement possible next week.

*Why it matters:* This is the first real-world use of the frontier-AI executive order, landing right as OpenAI floats giving Washington equity — the access fight and the ownership fight are the same negotiation.

## 5. Grok 4.5 shows up trained on Cursor's code
**xAI · June 28 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Musk says Grok 4.5, built on a 1.5-trillion-parameter foundation model, is now in private beta at SpaceX and Tesla, with Cursor's coding data folded in via supplemental training — the payoff from SpaceX's $60 billion acquisition of Cursor-maker Anysphere in June. Musk claims performance "close to, perhaps exceeding" Claude Opus, but xAI has published no benchmarks, and the public API still runs on the older Grok 4.3.

*Why it matters:* Unverified claims aside, this is the clearest signal yet of what SpaceX actually bought Cursor for — training data, not just distribution.

## Under the radar
Realta Fusion says it became the first commercial fusion company to convert plasma energy directly into electricity, skipping the steam turbine. The Wisconsin startup ran the test on its WHAM reactor June 19, powering lightbulbs at roughly 100 volts, and claims the method could reach 90% efficiency. *Why it matters:* Cutting the turbine is a genuine cost reduction for fusion plants, if the trick scales past a lightbulb.
