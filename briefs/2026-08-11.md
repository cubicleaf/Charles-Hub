*The AI compute crunch stopped being abstract this week — Apple is testing Chinese memory, Anthropic is offloading its data-center financing, and more than $21 billion in chip-industry capital moved in a single day.*

## 1. Apple tests Chinese CXMT memory chips for iPhones and MacBooks
**Apple · Aug 10 · 1 day ago**

Apple is testing DRAM from China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) in iPhones and MacBooks and has held early talks on supplying China-market devices, pending White House sign-off, per The Wall Street Journal. CXMT's chips already price near Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung, and HP and Acer already use them abroad. Senators led by Chuck Schumer have given Apple until August 21 to rule out Chinese memory entirely.

*Why it matters:* The AI-driven memory shortage is pushing Apple toward a supplier it's spent years avoiding, and Washington is watching.

## 2. Anthropic launches Theseus, a Macquarie- and GIC-backed data center venture
**Anthropic · Aug 10 · 1 day ago**

Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management, and Singapore's GIC formed Theseus Infrastructure, a platform where Macquarie and GIC fund and own AI data centers while Anthropic signs on as anchor tenant under long-term leases. It builds on Anthropic's 2025 pledge to put $50 billion into custom U.S. data centers and is expected to create thousands of construction and operating jobs at sites still to be identified.

*Why it matters:* Labs are separating the model business from capital-intensive real estate, pushing the AI buildout's financial risk onto asset managers and sovereign funds.

## 3. Meta returns to open weights with the 30B-parameter Muse Glimmer
**Meta · Aug 10 · 1 day ago**

Meta released Muse Glimmer, an Apache 2.0-licensed 30-billion-parameter model built for agentic and coding tasks on a single consumer GPU, scoring 76.0 on SWE-bench Verified and 51.2% on SWE-bench Pro — ahead of Google's Gemma4-31B on most benchmarks. Zuckerberg paired the release with a push for Washington to avoid restricting open-weight AI, warning Chinese developers would fill the gap otherwise.

*Why it matters:* Meta swinging back to open weights, backed by an explicit competitiveness argument aimed at Congress, turns "open vs. closed" into a policy fight.

## 4. Sony and TSMC commit $6.4B to a Japan image-sensor plant
**Sony / TSMC · Aug 10 · 1 day ago**

Sony Semiconductor Solutions and TSMC will jointly invest roughly ¥1 trillion ($6.4 billion) in a new image-sensor plant on Sony's Kumamoto campus, with Sony holding 60% and TSMC 40%, targeting 2029 production. Both are chasing demand from automotive and robotics customers, and Japan's government is weighing financial support.

*Why it matters:* The AI hardware race extends past GPUs and memory into the sensors autonomous machines depend on.

## 5. Intel raises $15B in stock to fund its AI chip build-out
**Intel · Aug 10 · 1 day ago**

Intel launched a $15 billion stock offering, with underwriters holding a 30-day option on $2.25 billion more, citing demand from AI compute, physical AI, and advanced packaging. The raise follows a year in which Intel's stock more than tripled and data-center revenue jumped 59% last quarter, though shares fell over 3% premarket on dilution concerns.

*Why it matters:* A legacy chipmaker needing $15 billion just to keep pace shows how capital-hungry the AI hardware race has become.
