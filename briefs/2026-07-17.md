*Today's theme: Beijing wrote an AI governance rulebook and shipped the world's largest open model in one day, while Washington's chip supply chain took another nine-figure bet.*

## 1. Xi Jinping unveils a China-led rival to US AI governance
**China · July 17 · Today**

Xi Jinping delivered the first keynote by a Chinese president at the World AI Conference in Shanghai, where 29 countries — including Russia, Pakistan, and Indonesia — signed a founding agreement for the World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO), a Shanghai-headquartered intergovernmental body, with UN chief António Guterres attending. Xi pledged 5,000 AI training slots for developing nations over five years, offered 30 countries a weather-forecasting tool, and called US export controls an "overstretching" of security concerns.

*Why it matters:* China's first formal institutional counter to US-led AI governance — a bid to set global rules while Washington still leans on bilateral export controls.

## 2. Kimi K3 becomes the largest open-weight model ever released
**Moonshot AI · July 16 · 1 day ago**

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight mixture-of-experts model with a new "Kimi Delta Attention" mechanism, a 1-million-token context window, and native visual understanding. On Artificial Analysis's GDPval-AA v2 benchmark it scored 1,687, third place behind Claude Fable 5 Max and GPT-5.6 Sol Max, ahead of Claude Opus 4.8. Full weights follow July 27.

*Why it matters:* The largest open-weight model in the world now benchmarks within striking distance of the closed frontier — from a Chinese lab, not a US one.

## 3. TSMC pledges another $100 billion, raises 2026 capex to $64 billion
**TSMC · July 16 · 1 day ago**

Days after a record quarter, TSMC committed another $100 billion to Arizona for at least four more 2-nanometer fabs and raised its 2026 capex forecast to $60-64 billion, up from $52-56 billion, alongside a sales-growth forecast above 40%.

*Why it matters:* The only leading-edge chipmaker keeps raising its own spending ceiling, still betting AI demand outruns whatever it builds next.

## 4. Apple Intelligence clears China, powered by Alibaba and Baidu
**Apple · July 16 · 1 day ago**

China's Cyberspace Administration approved Apple Intelligence for iPhones, iPads, and Macs in mainland China. Alibaba's Qwen and Baidu will power the China version instead of Apple's own models; Alibaba shares rose almost 5% and Baidu nearly 4% on the news.

*Why it matters:* Apple's biggest untapped AI market opens, but only by outsourcing the model layer to two Chinese rivals.

## 5. Microsoft ships its largest-ever Patch Tuesday: 570 flaws, two under attack
**Microsoft · July 14 · 3 days ago**

Microsoft's July Patch Tuesday fixed a record 570 flaws, including two zero-days under active exploitation — in Active Directory Federation Services and SharePoint Server — plus a disclosed BitLocker bypass. Microsoft credited an internal AI vulnerability-scanner for surfacing much of the volume and now recommends patching Windows 11 within three days.

*Why it matters:* AI is finding more bugs than it's making headlines for, and the resulting patch volume is becoming its own IT burden.
