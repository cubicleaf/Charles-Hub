## 1. TSMC posts its most profitable quarter ever
**TSMC · July 16 · Today**

TSMC reported Q2 revenue of $40.2 billion, up 36% year-over-year and above guidance, with net profit up 77.4% to NT$706.56 billion and gross margin at 67.7%. AI-chip demand (HPC) is now 66% of revenue; the newly ramping 2nm node already adds 3% of wafer revenue, and N3 capacity stays sold out.

*Why it matters:* The lone leading-edge fab just posted a record quarter it still can't fully meet demand for — AI hardware's bottleneck is fab capacity, not chip design.

## 2. IBM has its worst trading day ever on an AI budget shift
**IBM · July 14 · 2 days ago**

IBM shares fell 25% in a single session after preliminary Q2 revenue of $17.2 billion missed consensus and adjusted EPS landed at $2.93. CEO Arvind Krishna said the company "faltered" as clients diverted budgets toward AI infrastructure and away from software and consulting, with a memory-chip shortage pushing customers to prioritize hardware above all else.

*Why it matters:* The clearest signal yet that AI infrastructure spending is crowding out, not just adding to, enterprise IT budgets.

## 3. China recovers an orbital rocket booster with a net
**CASC · July 10 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

A Long March 10B's first stage was caught in a net strung across a recovery platform six minutes after stage separation, over 300 kilometers offshore — the first controlled recovery of an orbital-class booster using net capture rather than landing legs. China plans to refly the stage before year's end; the rocket is also slated to carry astronauts to the Moon before 2030.

*Why it matters:* China becomes only the second country to recover an orbital booster intact, narrowing SpaceX's decade-long lead in reusable launch.

## 4. General Fusion becomes the first publicly traded fusion company
**General Fusion · July 13 · 3 days ago**

General Fusion began trading on Nasdaq as GFUZ via reverse merger, rising 40% intraday from its $12.85 open. The deal brought roughly $150 million in cash — less than expected after heavy SPAC redemptions — atop over $600 million raised privately, targeting a first power plant around 2035. The debut landed the same week Bloomberg reported global fusion investment up 69% to $4.5 billion.

*Why it matters:* Fusion is starting to face public-market scrutiny instead of private-round optimism, a shift already reshaping AI and robotics.

## 5. Anthropic recruits a fintech CEO to fix its compute problem
**Anthropic · July 13 · 3 days ago**

Anthropic hired Tom Blomfield, co-founder of Monzo and a Y Combinator general partner, to join its compute team under chief compute officer Tom Brown, taking a leave from YC to do it. It follows recent hires of pretraining lead Andrej Karpathy and former Microsoft Azure infrastructure exec Eric Boyd, as Anthropic juggles a multi-year TPU commitment and gigawatts of incoming data-center capacity.

*Why it matters:* Anthropic is now recruiting operators who've run banks, not just researchers — compute has become a commercial problem, not only a technical one.
