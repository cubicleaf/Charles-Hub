## 1. Apple Sues OpenAI Over Trade Secrets
**Apple / OpenAI · July 10, 2026 · 2 days ago**

Apple filed suit against OpenAI in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging a coordinated scheme to steal hardware trade secrets tied to OpenAI's in-development consumer device. The complaint names Tang Tan — OpenAI's chief hardware officer and a 24-year Apple veteran — for allegedly directing job candidates still employed at Apple to bring "actual parts" to interviews, and Chang Liu, an eight-year Apple engineer who joined OpenAI in 2026, for failing to return a company laptop containing confidential documents. Apple says it warned OpenAI by letter in February with no response. OpenAI's statement: "We have no interest in other companies' trade secrets." OpenAI acquired Jony Ive's hardware startup io for $6.5 billion in 2025.

*Why it matters:* This is the first major trade-secret suit between two companies that were partners two years ago, and it puts OpenAI's hardware ambitions under federal scrutiny right as it prepares to ship its first device.

## 2. OpenAI Ships GPT-5.6 Family: Sol, Terra, Luna
**OpenAI · July 9, 2026 · 3 days ago**

OpenAI took GPT-5.6 to general availability across three tiers — Sol (flagship, tuned for biology, chemistry, and cybersecurity), Terra (mid-tier), and Luna (budget) — priced at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 per million input/output tokens, undercutting comparable rivals. The release followed voluntary safety evaluations with government regulators, adopted under a June executive order asking major AI labs to submit frontier models for review before launch, a push partly spurred by concern over Anthropic's cybersecurity-focused Mythos model.

*Why it matters:* Pre-release government safety review is now a live norm for frontier launches rather than a proposal — worth watching whether it survives past this cycle.

## 3. SK Hynix's $26.5B Nasdaq Debut Pops 13%
**SK Hynix · July 10, 2026 · 2 days ago**

SK Hynix, the world's second-largest memory chipmaker, closed its first Nasdaq trading day up 13% after pricing its ADR offering at $149 and raising $26.5 billion — the largest first-time listing by a foreign company in U.S. history. Demand for the U.S. tranche ran seven times available shares. The stock switches from ticker SKHYV to SKHY on July 14.

*Why it matters:* It's the clearest market signal yet that AI-driven memory demand is reshaping where chipmakers raise capital, not just where they sell.

## 4. China Nets a Rocket Booster at Sea
**CALT / China · July 10, 2026 · 2 days ago**

On the Long March 10B's maiden flight, its first stage deployed four hooks and was caught by a net strung across the recovery ship Linghang Zhe roughly 430 km downrange — the first successful net-based orbital booster recovery, and only the second nation, after the U.S., to recover an orbital-class booster under control. China says the same stage will refly before year's end.

*Why it matters:* A working reuse method that skips SpaceX's landing-leg approach narrows China's gap in launch cadence and cost.

## 5. Illinois Signs AI Safety Law
**Illinois · July 6, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Governor JB Pritzker signed the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, requiring model developers to publish frameworks for identifying "catastrophic risk" and report qualifying incidents within 72 hours. Illinois joins California and New York; the three states together account for roughly 40% of the U.S. AI market, effectively setting a de facto national standard absent federal rules.

*Why it matters:* State-by-state AI safety law is coalescing into a real compliance regime while Congress stays on the sidelines.
