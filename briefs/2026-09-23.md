*Today's throughline: AI safety moves from lab pledges to a UN Security Council floor, while the labs keep shipping and pricing faster than oversight can track.*

## 1. UN Security Council holds its first AI-safety-specific session
**UN Security Council · Sept 23 · Today**

France chairs the Council's first meeting focused specifically on AI safety risk, not AI's broader geopolitics. Briefers: Yoshua Bengio (co-chair, UN's independent AI science panel), OpenAI's Sam Altman (in person), Anthropic's Dario Amodei (remote), and Hugging Face's Clément Delangue. China's DeepSeek was also invited — the first time the Council has hosted frontier US and Chinese developers on a shared safety agenda, framed around "loss of human control."

*Why it matters:* AI safety has moved from industry talking point to formal great-power diplomacy, with the labs themselves in the room.

## 2. OpenAI launches GPT-6 Sol and Luna, cuts API prices roughly in half
**OpenAI · Sept 22 · 1 day ago**

OpenAI's second and third model launches in three weeks, after flagship GPT-6 Astra. Sol targets sustained agentic work; Luna is a low-cost model for background tasks. API pricing fell roughly 50% versus GPT-5.6 rates: Sol now $2/$10 per million input/output tokens, Luna $0.10/$0.50. Third-party benchmarks this week put Sol ahead of Claude Opus 5 on some reasoning and long-horizon evals, at lower cost per task.

*Why it matters:* Frontier labs are now competing on cost-per-task as hard as capability, squeezing every agent-building company downstream.

## 3. OpenAI opens model training to outside safety evaluators
**OpenAI · Sept 22 · 1 day ago**

OpenAI will let outside groups — reportedly including METR and Redwood Research, no deal finalized — assess safety during training, not just before release. Priority areas: safety cases spanning training through deployment, critical safeguards, capability evals, and independent misalignment investigations.

*Why it matters:* OpenAI is moving toward the earlier, independent verification safety researchers have pushed for, ahead of being required to.

## 4. SpaceX stacks Starship for its first fully orbital flight
**SpaceX · Sept 21 · 2 days ago**

Super Heavy arrived at the Starbase, Texas pad ahead of Starship's first fully orbital test, targeted for September 28 pending FAA approval — the first FAA orbital license for a vehicle this size. The flight plans to deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, its first operational payload.

*Why it matters:* An orbital flight with a working payload turns Starship from a test program into an operational launch system.

## 5. Banks warn AI shopping agents raise fraud and privacy risks
**NatWest, Bank of America, Capital One and others · Sept 22 · 1 day ago**

A coalition including NatWest, Bank of America, ING, ASB Bank and Capital One told regulators that AI shopping agents — bots that enter payment details and buy on a customer's behalf — create new scam, fraud and privacy exposure, including impersonation of agents and merchants. They want mandatory disclosure when an agent is part of a transaction. Retailer John Lewis said agent-originated searches rose to 2.5% of traffic, up from 0.3% a year earlier.

*Why it matters:* Agentic commerce is scaling faster than fraud infrastructure built for human shoppers.

## Under the radar
**MIT's tiny flying robot gets 450% faster with a new AI controller.** A two-part control system boosted the insect-scale robot's speed ~450% and acceleration ~250%, enough for 10 somersaults in 11 seconds despite wind. *Why it matters:* Better control software, not new hardware, is unlocking insect-like agility in machines small enough to search disaster rubble.

**AI helped close the last open case of a 40-year-old symmetry problem.** Mathematicians used AI agents to construct the missing polynomial for the M23 sporadic group's inverse Galois problem, unsolved since the 1980s; a separate crowdsourced project catalogued all 25,000 cases of a related problem. *Why it matters:* AI accelerating pure-math research that had stalled for decades.
