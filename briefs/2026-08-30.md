*The fight over who controls AI infrastructure — models, chips, and legal leverage — dominated the last 48 hours.*

## 1. Nvidia Nears $12.9 Billion Deal for Hugging Face
**Nvidia · Aug 27 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

Nvidia has reportedly agreed to buy Hugging Face, the hub roughly 13 million developers use to share open-source AI models, for about $12.9 billion, per The Information, echoed by Bloomberg and CNBC. No contract is signed and talks could collapse; Hugging Face turned down a $500 million Nvidia investment last year valuing it at $7 billion. With DGX Cloud scaled back, the deal would give Nvidia a distribution chokepoint for open models plus a route back into cloud.

*Why it matters:* whoever owns where developers get their models gains leverage that has nothing to do with chip performance.

## 2. OpenAI Cuts Off Cursor's Model Access After SpaceX Buyout
**OpenAI · Aug 29 · 1 day ago**

OpenAI says Cursor loses direct access to its models on November 12, following SpaceX's completed $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor's parent, Anysphere, on August 14. OpenAI cited past terms-of-service breaches by Musk-controlled companies, naming Twitter/X and xAI. Cursor's CEO downplayed the hit, noting OpenAI models handle only about 5% of its traffic; Anthropic has pledged more Claude support to cover the gap.

*Why it matters:* the Altman-Musk rivalry is now directly reshaping which models power a coding tool millions of developers use daily.

## 3. Judge Rules Pentagon's Anthropic Blacklist Illegal
**Anthropic · Aug 27 · 3 days ago**

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's labeling of Anthropic a "supply-chain risk" was unlawful First Amendment retaliation and denied due process. The label, imposed after Anthropic refused to let the military use Claude for domestic surveillance or autonomous weapons, had blocked it from federal contracts. The government is expected to appeal.

*Why it matters:* sets precedent that an AI lab can refuse a military use case without being cut off from government business.

## 4. NASA's Roman Space Telescope Set for Falcon Heavy Launch
**NASA / SpaceX · Aug 30 · Today**

NASA's $4.3 billion Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is scheduled to lift off on a Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center at 7:26 a.m. ET, with roughly even odds on weather and a Monday backup window. Roman will survey dark energy and exoplanets over a field of view far wider than Hubble's.

*Why it matters:* a rare non-AI story — Roman reshapes how much sky astronomers can survey per observation, not just what they can zoom into.

## 5. Marvell's Google Chip Deal Could Reach $120 Billion — With Fine Print
**Marvell · Aug 27 · 3 days ago**

Marvell disclosed a custom-silicon deal with Google for chips built around its TPU ecosystem, paired with a warrant for Google to buy up to roughly 58.97 million Marvell shares at $206.58 each — about $12.2 billion if fully exercised. The widely quoted $120 billion figure is revenue Marvell could earn only if Google hits every purchase threshold through fiscal 2033; the filing calls the purchases discretionary, not committed. Marvell shares fell about 8%.

*Why it matters:* custom-silicon deals are now standard alongside GPU purchases, but "up to $120 billion" headlines do a lot of work.
