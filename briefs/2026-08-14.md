*Today's theme: the AI buildout is consolidating — through acquisitions, memory capacity, and a push to write AI's safety rules before Washington writes its own.*

## 1. Anthropic in talks to buy Decart AI for $6 billion
**Anthropic / Decart · Aug 13, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Anthropic is negotiating to acquire Israeli startup Decart AI for about $6 billion — its largest acquisition yet, roughly 50% above the $4 billion valuation Decart set two months ago. Decart builds "world models" that simulate physical environments and software that cuts AI training costs via chip efficiency; its real-time video model, Lucy, already powers e-commerce virtual try-ons. Talks aren't final.

*Why it matters:* Anthropic is buying its way out of a compute bottleneck ahead of a widely expected IPO.

## 2. Google's Gemini app crosses 1 billion monthly users
**Google · Aug 11, 2026 · 3 days ago · Still developing**

Google says Gemini passed 1 billion monthly active users, its fastest-growing product ever, climbing from roughly 950 million to over a billion in under a month. Google now counts 14 products with a billion-plus users; 63% of Gemini interactions happen by voice, and the app generates more than 150 million images a day. ChatGPT reportedly hit the same mark in June and still leads overall, but its growth is said to be slowing while Gemini gains through Android, Chrome, and Search.

*Why it matters:* Distribution through an OS, a browser, and a search bar proved as decisive as model quality.

## 3. DeepMind's Hassabis pitches Treasury on a FINRA for AI
**Google DeepMind · Aug 13, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Demis Hassabis has personally briefed Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and White House tech-policy chief Michael Kratsios on his proposal for an industry-funded AI watchdog modeled on FINRA, the body that polices Wall Street under SEC oversight. Frontier labs would share models with independent reviewers up to 30 days before release, screening for dangerous cyber, biological, and deception capabilities. Treasury has reportedly been building a similar concept in parallel.

*Why it matters:* A proposal that started as an essay on X is now a live negotiation with the officials who'd charter it.

## 4. SK Hynix commits $720 billion to triple AI memory capacity
**SK Hynix · Aug 13, 2026 · 1 day ago**

SK Hynix, which holds 58% of the market for the high-bandwidth memory chips that feed AI accelerators, is spending $720 billion to triple production capacity across South Korea and the U.S. by 2034. The buildout follows its Nasdaq debut in July; its market cap has since passed $1 trillion, more than five times its value a year ago. South Korea's president is pushing SK Hynix and rival Samsung to add capacity faster, backed by a government chip package worth at least $22 billion.

*Why it matters:* Memory supply, not GPU count, is becoming the real ceiling on AI compute.

## 5. Astranis unveils a satellite built to watch other satellites
**Astranis · Aug 13, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Astranis unveiled Perceptor, its first spacecraft built for space domain awareness — tracking and flying by other satellites in geostationary orbit, about 22,236 miles up. It's built on Astranis's existing MicroGEO bus, which already has five satellites flying commercially in GEO, giving it in-orbit heritage rival bidders lack. Multiple units could watch one target from different angles at once, making it harder to evade observation.

*Why it matters:* A commercial broadband satellite maker is moving directly into military space-surveillance work.
