*Frontier AI policy and frontier AI products moved in lockstep this week, with Washington's hand visible in nearly every major release.*

## 1. Anthropic ships Sonnet 5, Commerce lifts the Fable/Mythos freeze
**Anthropic · July 1, 2026 · 1 day ago**

Claude Sonnet 5 rolled out June 30 and became the default model for Free and Pro users on July 1, priced at an introductory $2/$10 per million input/output tokens, with performance close to Opus 4.8. The same day, Commerce lifted the export-control freeze it had imposed June 12 over a jailbreak vulnerability, letting Anthropic restore global access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 after adding new cybersecurity classifiers.

*Why it matters:* A model launch and a national-security climbdown landing on the same day shows how tightly frontier AI releases are now bound to US export policy.

## 2. OpenAI previews GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna — under government gate
**OpenAI · June 26, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

OpenAI unveiled a three-tier GPT-5.6 family — Sol for hard coding and security work, Terra for business workloads, Luna for cheap everyday tasks — but capped initial access to roughly 20 government-vetted organizations under a White House order requiring a 30-day safety review before wider release. Sol's "ultra" mode reportedly scores 91.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and runs at up to 750 tokens/sec on Cerebras hardware for select customers.

*Why it matters:* It's the first frontier launch gated by a formal US pre-release safety review — a template regulators may now reuse on every major lab.

## 3. Pax Silica chip alliance grows to 24 nations
**US State Department · June 25–26, 2026 · 6 days ago · Still developing**

Ten more governments — including the EU, Germany, the Netherlands, and Kazakhstan — signed the Pax Silica Declaration at the second Washington summit, bringing the US-led semiconductor and AI supply-chain pact to 24 members. The State Department also piloted "Pax Pass," a credentialing platform in Panama meant to fast-track vetted chip and AI-infrastructure shipments; China remains excluded.

*Why it matters:* The alliance is becoming the main mechanism for deciding who gets advanced AI chips and who doesn't.

## 4. Bending Spoons raises $1.68B in Nasdaq debut
**Bending Spoons · July 1, 2026 · 1 day ago**

The Milan holding company that owns AOL, Vimeo, Evernote, and WeTransfer priced its IPO at $29 a share, above its marketed range, raising $1.68 billion at roughly an $18–19 billion valuation. Shares rose 11% on their first trading day on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker BSP.

*Why it matters:* It's the biggest tech IPO of the year so far and a test of investor appetite for consumer-software roll-ups outside the AI-infrastructure trade.

## 5. Nvidia launches Halos for Robotics
**Nvidia · June 22, 2026 · 10 days ago · Still developing**

Nvidia introduced a full-stack safety architecture for humanoid and industrial robots — IGX Thor compute, a Halos OS safety layer, and an ANAB-accredited inspection lab — built on 18,600-plus engineering-years from its self-driving safety work. Agility Robotics' Digit is the first commercial humanoid certified under the stack, as Figure AI separately reports one robot built per hour at its BotQ factory.

*Why it matters:* Safety certification, not raw capability, is becoming the bottleneck standing between humanoid robots and factory floors.

## Under the radar
**UN launches "AI for Good" governance commission — Axios · July 1, 2026 · 1 day ago.** The UN and ITU convened a new AI governance body co-chaired by Salesforce's Marc Benioff and Rwanda's Paul Kagame, with members including Jensen Huang, Andy Jassy, and Brad Smith; it meets first on July 8 in Geneva. It's now one of three competing tracks — alongside Pax Silica and national AI-safety institutes — for who sets global AI rules.

**Qualcomm to acquire Modular for ~$3.9B — June 26, 2026 · 6 days ago.** The all-stock deal pairs Qualcomm's mobile chip business with Modular's AI-inference software stack, nine months after Modular raised $250M at a $1.6B valuation — a sign chipmakers are paying up for software that squeezes more inference out of existing silicon.
